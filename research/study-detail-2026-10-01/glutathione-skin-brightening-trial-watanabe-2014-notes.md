## 4. Why no how-it-works block

Malcolm asked for blocks on "the proven working active effects of what was tested". The brief allows a block only for an effect tested in
this paper or in a laboratory study read at source, said in laboratory terms and in the register's words. **For oxidised glutathione (GSSG),
the form this trial used, no tested effect qualifies.** These are the candidates I considered, and why each fails:

| Candidate block                                                                          | Tested where                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Why it is out                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **C1. "In the laboratory, glutathione slows tyrosinase, the enzyme that makes pigment"** | Cell-free enzyme tests: Matsuki 2008 (PMID 18670186, _Yakugaku Zasshi_, in Japanese; English abstract read: "Glutathione dose dependently inhibited melanin synthesis in the reaction of tyrosinase and L-DOPA"); Jara 1988 (cited by Watanabe; not read) | (a) The register §4 avoid list bans "Inhibits melanin production / tyrosinase in your skin" and adds: "The mechanism is also unproven for GSSG in vivo: Watanabe calls it 'no more than speculation'." A block whose only message is "glutathione blocks the pigment enzyme", placed under "How does glutathione work on skin?", reads as what happens in the reader's skin whatever its first words say. (b) It tests **reduced** glutathione (GSH), not the trial's GSSG. (c) **In living pigment cells the independent evidence goes the other way:** Chung et al. 2016 (PMID 27128906, PMC4881455, academic, full text read): "Surprisingly, GSH had no inhibitory effect on melanogenesis: the melanin content did not change at any concentration tested", and "GSH did not affect intracellular activity of tyrosinase".
