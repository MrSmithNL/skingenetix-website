## 4. How it works: what was tested, and what I propose

**Tested laboratory evidence for Argireline® itself, read at source:**

1. **Blanes-Mira 2002 (developers' study).** The abstract states that Argireline "significantly inhibited neurotransmitter release" and that
   this "was due to the interference of the hexapeptide with the formation and/or stability of ... the vesicular fusion (known as SNARE)
   complex". The cell type, doses and methods are in the paywalled full text, **which I could not read**. Wang 2013's introduction and
   discussion (pp. 147, 150) restate the same finding and add that the peptide is "patterned after the N-terminal domain of SNAP-25 (aa
   12–17)".
2. **Lim 2018 (independent, open access).** It tested unmodified Argireline ("Arg0") on neurons grown from human dental-pulp stem cells:
   glutamate release fell about 13%, and **Figure 5B marks it as not significant** (only the modified analogue Arg3, −43%, carries a star).
   So it cannot support a positive block. (Its introduction also repeats the "up to 48%" misreading the register warns about.)
3. **Kraeling 2015 (US FDA, skin samples).** This is a penetration finding, not an effect: most of the peptide washed off; 0.22% of the dose
   was in the outermost layer of human skin and 0.01% in the epidermis, with none in the dermis. It is honest context for why the register
   keeps the mechanism "framed as laboratory findings". It is not a block.
4. **Wang 2013 JCLT (aged mice, type I collagen up).** This is an animal study, and the register lists "boosts collagen" under AVOID. Not
   used.

**Decision: one block, on this page only.** Wang is the crow's-feet trial, the expression-line site the peptide was designed for, and the
paper's own introduction is built on this laboratory work. Raikou and Tadini get **zero** blocks (reasons in their notes), so no text
repeats across pages.
