**Things in the paper a reviewer may trip over (none changes a number we use):**

- **55.8% cannot be recomputed from Table 2's means** ((24.1 − 15.0) / 15.0 = 60.7%), nor 31.6% (gives 29.0%). The Methods say every
  percentage is an average of each woman's own change, and Table 3's footnote b defines the improvement as the average within each group.
  Keep the paper's figures; do not "correct" them.
- Table 3's volume differences are printed as "−0.3 m³" and "−0.4 m³", a units misprint, so no absolute volume is quoted anywhere.
- Table 1 calls both units µg/ml, but the elastin values (200–271) sit far above the stated standard-curve range (12.5–50 µg/ml), and the
  96-hour collagen values touch the top of theirs (5–15). The proposal quotes relative changes only.
- The Discussion says MMP1 and MMP2 rose "at all the tested concentrations"; the Results and Figure 1 say only at 0.01 nM. The Conclusion
  says "0.01–10 nM"; the strengths tested were 0.01, 1 and 100 nM.
- No photograph grading, no self-assessment and no responder percentages beyond "all participants using NRFS" exist in this paper, so there
  is nothing else to fold into a result section.

| o3  | Depth vs plain serum, 32.8% more (−20.3% vs −15.3%)                | 0.0123   | Yes, plain photograph                                                                                                                                                 |
| —   | Depth vs the Matrixyl 3000 product, 23.4% more                     | 0.0577   | **No.** Not significant; register §1 claim 2(a) and §4 forbid it as a win. It stays where it already is, in the limits section, in the template's positive-first form |

**Order:** strongest first by the strength of the evidence (p < 0.0001, then 0.0044, then 0.0123). The Matrixyl result goes second although
it shares a comparator with neither neighbour, because beating an active product is the stronger statement for a reader deciding whether to
buy, and its p-value is the stronger of the two.

**Depth gets its own section (decision asked for in the task).** Reasons:

1. The template rule decides it: §3.1 gives a section to every result that is positive and significant against the comparison, and depth vs
   the plain serum (p = 0.0123) is one. Malcolm asked for "separate content sections for each of the proven trial outcomes".
2. It answers a different reader question. Volume is "how much has the crease shrunk overall"; depth is "how deep is the line". A reader
   searching the second question gets a heading that answers it.
3. It does not repeat the volume section, because the three sections split the method between them: o1 carries the set-up (skin check, ten
   cosmetic-free days, the chosen wrinkle, the split face) and the responder line; o2 carries the blinding, the positive control and the
   time course; o3 carries how PRIMOS works.
4. The before/after picture can only be used once, and it belongs with the larger, more significant volume result. Depth takes a plain
   photograph of the measuring method, which is what the template prescribes for an instrument reading.

The case for folding depth into o1 (same wrinkle, same 20 women, same instrument) is real, and if the design critic finds o1 and o3
repetitive on the rendered page, the fallback is one extra sentence in o1's second paragraph: "Its depth fell 20.3% against 15.3%, a 32.8%
greater reduction (p = 0.0123)." Delete o3 in that case.

**Not used, available if wanted:** Table 3 gives the depth gap as −28.3 µm (the GHK-Cu side's crease lost on average 28.3 thousandths of a
millimetre more depth than the plain side's). It is precise and true, but it reads small next to the percentages, so it is left out under
the "strongest credible claim" rule. Malcolm's call.

## 5. Considered and not proposed

**A third block from this paper: the TIMP1 "brake".** It was tested here (it is in the paper's title), but **no register line covers it**,
so it cannot pass the "quote the register line" test until the register gains one. The honest version also has to carry its mixed side.
Draft, for Malcolm and a register addition:

> _Title:_ "The same cells raised their brake on collagen-cutting enzymes" _Body:_ "In the laboratory, after one day with GHK-Cu, the
> fibroblasts made more TIMP1, the instruction for a protein that holds back the enzymes that cut collagen, at all three strengths (p <
> 0.05). It rose by more than MMP1, the main collagen-cutting enzyme, by 2.7 to 3.8 times, a balance the authors link to the extra collagen
> the cells released."

Source: Figure 1 (TIMP1 ≈ 5.7×, 3.4× and 2.6× control, read from the bars; all marked p<0.05) and Table 4 (TIMP1/MMP1 3.76, 2.65, 3.1). The
other side, which the block would leave out: at 0.01 nM MMP1 and MMP2 also rose (p = 0.03); TIMP2 fell at 1 and 100 nM; this is gene
activity (mRNA) after 24 hours, and the authors note that TIMPs act on the enzymes directly, not through gene activity. Gene-activity
wording also sits close to the register's avoid row on "resets genes". **Recommendation: hold.**

**Pickart 2015 (the review the config's context cites).** Read in full; **no block from it.** (1) Its authors are Skin Biology, a GHK-Cu
seller (register §3 row 17), and the brief rules out a supplier's assertion. (2) Its laboratory statements are one-line summaries of other
papers with no numbers or conditions (for example "GHK-Cu stimulated collagen, dermatan sulfate, chondroitin sulfate, and a small
proteoglycan, decorin"); using them properly means reading Maquart 1988, Wegrowski 1992 and Siméon 1999/2000, none of which is in the
register. (3) Most of the review is in avoid-list language: wound healing, "resetting DNA to a healthier state", DNA repair,
anti-inflammatory, cancer and lung disease. (4) It is not what this trial measured.

**Mokhtar 2026** ("Preclinical data consistently demonstrated that GHK-Cu enhances extracellular matrix synthesis (type I collagen and
glycosaminoglycans)", register §8.1) is abstract-only and would restate m1. **Kang 2009** (keratinocyte markers) is on the hub, is not what
this trial measured, and its "proliferative potential" sits near "stimulates cell growth".
