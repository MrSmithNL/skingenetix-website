## Five decisions that apply to all nine

1. **Trial figures on product pages (your decision 15).** Your rule since 2026-09-26 keeps trial percentages on the
   ingredient pages until the formula sheet confirms our concentrations. The live product pages still carry them
   ("55.8%", "7 in 10", "+256% in lab tests", "up to 23%", "nearly 1 in 2"). The drafts follow the rule: they keep the
   trial's design, size, length, direction and timing, and send readers to the ingredient page for the numbers. Each
   removed figure is listed in its draft. **Choose:** keep them off (drafts as written), or put them back on the products
   that you confirm the formula for.
2. **The "free from" line.** Every live product says "No parabens · no mineral oil · no synthetic fragrance". The EU's
   guidance on cosmetic claims (Technical Document, Annex III) does not accept "free from parabens", because it
   disparages permitted ingredients. The drafts remove the line. The PDRN register still lists it as an always-true fact,
   so it needs your call. The Ingredients page banner says the same ("free from parabens… mineral oil").
3. **Keyword ownership (your decision 3).** Three drafts give an alternative title for the open items: 3a (Argireline in
   the title), 3b (the Matrixyl cream on "skin firming cream"), 3c (the Matrixyl serum on "firming serum", the glutathione
   serum on "brightening serum"). The writer advises against "peptide cream" for the Matrixyl cream: the audit judged it
   not winnable for this page.
4. **The formula sheet (your decision 8).** Concentrations are stated only where the register confirms them: PDRN 1% and
   GHK-Cu 2%. The drafts do not state what "10% MATRIXYL" on the serum pack means, the Argireline grade, or glutathione
   "2% GSSG". The glutathione product title, label and ingredient list say 2% today; the register makes that conditional on
   the supplier's certificate.
5. **"Assessed as safe" on the Matrixyl pages.** The independent Cosmetic Ingredient Review panel's own words about the
   two Matrixyl 3000 peptides ("assessed as safe in cosmetics at current use levels") appear in the research text and the
   safety answer only, attributed, with "not a test of this product". Keep or strike.

## Product facts to check on the cartons (found while drafting)

- **Argireline serum:** its live ingredient list includes palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and tetrapeptide-7, the Matrixyl 3000
  pair. The Matrixyl register suspects two products' lists were swapped.
- **Matrixyl cream:** its ingredient list may end in acetyl hexapeptide-8; elastin is on the pack and in the live FAQ but
  not in the list (the draft leaves elastin out); the collagen's source species is undisclosed (the draft adds an allergy
  line).
- **Copper night cream:** its live copy names centella, beta-glucan, squalane and jojoba, which its ingredient list does not
  show. The full ingredient list is needed to finish its benefits.
- **PDRN products:** beta-glucan (serum) and squalane (cream) in the benefits; whether the cream's collagen is "of animal
  origin" (the live FAQ says so; the register does not).
- **Vegan:** the Ingredients page says every formula is vegan; the Matrixyl cream's FAQ says it is not.
- **Pack wording:** the render briefs print "Repair" and "Repairing" on some packs; the registers avoid "repairs".
- **Reviews:** two displayed reviews use condition wording ("fades my acne scars", "rosacea"); keep them out of curated
  carousels.
