## Part 4 — The two constraints that decide what we may say

### 4.1 Regulation (research, not legal advice; `research-regulation-and-safety.md`)

- **US.** The FDA decides whether a microneedling product is a medical device by its claims and by whether the needles are
  meant to reach living skin. "Stimulates collagen", "treats wrinkles, scars or acne", "promotes healing" and "penetrates
  deep" make a stamp a device; "helps exfoliate", "improves the appearance of skin" and "smoother look" do not. The FDA has
  authorised no microneedling device for over-the-counter sale and none for use with another product, and it describes
  microneedling products sold with instructions to apply a topical product as possible combination products. **Our sets
  (a 0.5 mm stamp sold with serum vials and "stamp, then apply" use) match that description.**
- **EU.** Microneedling is not among the MDR's Annex XVI products; a stamp with appearance-only claims is a consumer
  product under the General Product Safety Regulation. The serum is a cosmetic, and cosmetics law is written for intact
  skin: a serum meant for freshly needled skin would need a safety assessment that covers that use.
- **UK.** MHRA follows the maker's stated purpose; the advertising regulator upheld complaints against a derma roller
  ("10 years younger", before-and-after photos, 2012) and an at-home device ("clinically proven to lift 20%", May 2026).
- **Live today on our store and on the cannot list:** "absorb deeper" on both stamp pages and "through fine, sterile
  micro-channels" on the collection.

**What we may say** (all markets): helps exfoliate; improves the look of skin; smoother look and feel; factual product
specifications; plain safety and hygiene information. **What we may not:** collagen, scars, wrinkle treatment, healing,
penetration or absorption claims, "clinical", "medical grade", "safe", "sterile" without a test, "alternative to
injections", unproven percentages, and before-and-after photographs without signed proof.

**The two defensible postures for the sets:**

- **A. Decouple (lowest risk, available now):** the stamp is sold for exfoliation and a smoother look; the serum is used
  on clean, intact skin, never pushed into freshly stamped skin; no page or leaflet gives a "stamp, then apply the vial"
  sequence.
- **B. Keep the pairing, but earn it:** a cosmetic safety assessment that covers application right after micro-puncture,
  sterile single-dose vials with batch sterility testing, human patch testing and an allergen review of PDRN and GHK-Cu,
  and a documented position on the 0.5 mm question with a regulatory specialist. Until then, no "sterile" and no "designed
  for use after microneedling".

### 4.2 Evidence (`research-clinical-evidence.md`)

No controlled trial of an at-home facial stamp exists; a 2026 review rates home microneedling evidence Level D. The nearest
controlled trials are clinician-applied (a 0.25 mm roller with saline did not change eye-area wrinkles; a 0.5 mm roller
plus an active beat saline), and no human trial tests PDRN, GHK-Cu or peptides with home needling. Rare but real harm
is documented: granulomas after products were pushed into needled skin, viral spread from a home roller, a facial ulcer
after an at-home device with an oil. Barrier function recovers in about 1 to 3 days after short needles; trials mostly
spaced sessions 2 weeks apart.

**Why this is good news for ranking:** no page in the top 3 for any microneedling term says any of this. The honest,
sourced page is the one the AI answers can cite (Part 3), and the one Google's quality raters are told to reward on health
topics. Our edge is the truth told precisely, plus the two trials we already appraise (Yogya 2022, Li 2015).

## Part 7 — Decisions that are yours

1. **Serum posture (unblocks the how-to on every page).** A: decouple, the serum on intact skin only, never pushed into
   freshly stamped skin (lowest risk, available now). B: keep the pairing and first commission the cosmetic safety
   assessment that covers use right after micro-puncture, sterile single-dose fill with batch testing, and patch testing.
   **Recommended: A on every page now, and commission B in parallel if you want the paired format later.**
