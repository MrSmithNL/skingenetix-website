## How to use

Use in the morning as the last leave-on step, after your serums. Warm a pea-sized amount and massage lightly
over the face and neck. Always follow with SPF 30+, then makeup if you wish; the lightweight, fast-absorbing
texture sits comfortably underneath.

If you separately use pure L-ascorbic acid vitamin C, strong acids (AHA or BHA) or a high-strength retinol,
apply those at a different time of day.

## Clinical research

**GHK-Cu, the copper peptide in this gel-cream, has a 12-week face-cream study and a double-blind trial behind it.**

A face cream containing a copper peptide was studied for 12 weeks in 71 women and presented at the American
Academy of Dermatology meeting in 2002. The reported results were improved firmness, clarity and appearance, with fewer
fine lines. It is a conference abstract and was not published in full.

In a randomised, double-blind, split-face trial of a GHK-Cu serum:

- 40 women aged 40 to 65 used it twice a day for 8 weeks (39 were analysed)
- Wrinkle volume and depth fell further with the GHK-Cu serum than with the plain serum base, measured by 3D
  skin imaging of a crow's-feet wrinkle
- Change was measured at week 4 and had grown by week 8
- It was well tolerated by 39 of the 40 women; one had a minor reaction, stopped, and it settled without
  treatment

Also on record:

- In a one-month skin-biopsy study of 20 volunteers, more people using a copper-binding peptide cream showed an
  increase in procollagen (the precursor of collagen) than in the other three creams tested (10 people per
  cream, thigh skin, no statistical test between the creams)
- An independent 2026 systematic review of 20 studies, two of them randomised trials, concluded that GHK-Cu
  significantly reduced wrinkle volume and depth compared with controls. It also noted a limited number of
  well-designed clinical trials
- No published study has tested GHK-Cu together with vitamin C in people

Sources: Leyden et al. 2002, American Academy of Dermatology 60th Annual Meeting, abstract P68 (as reported in
published reviews; the abstract itself is unread); Badenhorst et al. 2016, J Aging Sci 4(3):166 (the GHK-Cu
serum used a lipid nano-carrier, and the plain serum base carried neither the peptide nor the carrier, so the
difference covers both; funded by the test serum's maker, with independent statistics); Abdulghani et al. 1998,
Disease Management and Clinical Outcomes; Mokhtar et al. 2026, Aesthetic Surgery Journal. Results describe the
ingredient as studied, not this product; the full figures are on the research page.

## FAQ

### What does GHK-Cu do for skin, and what does this day gel-cream add?

GHK-Cu is a copper peptide your own body makes, first isolated from human plasma in 1973. In a double-blind
trial, a GHK-Cu serum reduced crow's-feet wrinkle volume and depth further than the plain serum base after
8 weeks, and an independent 2026 review reached the same conclusion on wrinkle volume and depth. The human
evidence is still a handful of trials. This gel-cream carries 2% GHK-Cu with a stable vitamin C and
hyaluronic acid in a light texture for firmer-looking, smoother-looking skin under SPF.

### Does vitamin C work with copper peptides in this gel-cream?

This gel-cream contains 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, a stable form of vitamin C, in the same formula as 2%
GHK-Cu. No published study has tested GHK-Cu with vitamin C in people, so we make no claim that the pair works
better together. If you separately use pure L-ascorbic acid vitamin C, apply it at a different time of day.

### What should you not mix copper peptides with?

No combination has been tested in a trial. Pure L-ascorbic acid vitamin C, strong acids (AHA or BHA) and
high-strength retinol are best used at a different time of day. Copper peptides are not acids or exfoliants,
so they layer easily with other peptides, hyaluronic acid and moisturisers. Introduce one new product at a
time.

### How often should you use copper peptides on your face?

Use this gel-cream once a day, in the morning, after your serum, then SPF. The trial applied a GHK-Cu serum
twice a day and measured change at weeks 4 and 8, so judge any copper peptide over at least 8 weeks of
regular use. For the evening, the Copper Peptide (GHK-Cu) 2% Night Cream is the richer partner.

### Does it replace sunscreen?

No. It does not contain SPF. Always apply a separate broad-spectrum SPF 30+ as the last step of your morning
routine.

### What are the downsides of copper peptides, and should I patch test?

In the 8-week controlled trial, 39 of 40 women tolerated a GHK-Cu serum well, with none of the peeling,
dryness or redness seen with retinoids. One woman had a minor reaction on both sides of her face, stopped,
and it settled without treatment. That trial did not state its concentration, and our 2% products have no
tolerance study of their own, so patch test first: apply a small amount behind the ear for 24 to 48 hours.
Stop using it if stinging or redness does not settle, and see a pharmacist or dermatologist if a reaction
persists. Keep it away from the eyes and broken skin. It has not been tested in pregnancy or while
breastfeeding, so ask your doctor or pharmacist first.
