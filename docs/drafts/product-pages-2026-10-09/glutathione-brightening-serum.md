## Clinical research

Field `custom.clinical_research`:

Topical glutathione has a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial behind it. The serum is built around
glutathione, with a
stable form of vitamin C and niacinamide. The trial tested the glutathione only.

- Design: 30 women aged 30 to 50, skin types III and IV. A lotion with 2% oxidised glutathione (GSSG) went on
  one half of
  the face and the same lotion without it on the other half, twice daily for 10 weeks.
- Brightening: the instrument reading of skin pigment fell more on the glutathione side than on the placebo
  side. More
  women, and the investigator, rated the glutathione side as moderately brighter at week 10.
- Timing: the pigment reading was already significantly lower on the glutathione side from week 1.
- Secondary results: crow's feet looked visibly softer on the glutathione side in a group of women, and none on the
  placebo side. Skin texture measured smoother from week 6.
- Tolerability: all 30 women completed 10 weeks of twice-daily use, with no reactions attributed to glutathione.

Watanabe et al. 2014, Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol. The trial was run by the manufacturer of the ingredient
(Kyowa Hakko
Bio). A separate, independent trial of a glutathione and vitamin C serum (Wahab et al. 2021, 23 women, 8 weeks)
found the
same direction. Results describe the ingredient as studied, not this product; the full figures are on the
[glutathione research page](/pages/glutathione-research).

## FAQ

Seven items: six on the questions people ask, plus one safety item (the last).

### What does a glutathione serum do for skin?

Glutathione is a tripeptide antioxidant that the body makes in its own cells, used in skincare for
brighter-looking skin.
In a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 30 women, a lotion with oxidised glutathione made the treated
half of the
face look brighter than the placebo half over 10 weeks. This serum adds a stable vitamin C, niacinamide and
hyaluronic acid.
Read [our appraisal of the trial](/blogs/clinical-studies/glutathione-skin-brightening-trial-watanabe-2014).

### Does topical glutathione work for brightening skin?

In the two controlled trials of topical glutathione, the treated side of the face looked brighter or read lower
for skin
pigment than the placebo side. The first (30 women, 10 weeks) was run by the ingredient's manufacturer. The
second, a
glutathione and vitamin C serum (23 women, 8 weeks), declared no funding and no conflicts. A 2025 systematic review
concluded that topical glutathione "shows promise". Neither trial tested our serum, so they describe the ingredient.
See the [glutathione research page](/pages/glutathione-research).

### How do I use a glutathione serum, and when should I apply it?

Apply 3-4 drops after cleansing, before your moisturiser, morning and evening. In the morning, always finish
with SPF. Use
it for the full 10 weeks the trial ran before you judge it.

### How long does a glutathione serum take to work?

In the trial, the pigment reading was significantly lower from week 1, which an instrument measures and the eye
may not
see. The first changes the women themselves could see were reported around week 4, and the trial ran for 10
weeks. Reviews
describe the brightening as temporary, so keep using it to keep the look.

### Can I use it with vitamin C or niacinamide?

It already contains both: a stable vitamin C (3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid) and niacinamide, so there is nothing to
add. It
layers under moisturiser, and we pair it with the
[Copper Peptide (GHK-Cu) Day Gel-Cream](/products/copper-peptide-ghk-cu-day-gel-cream). No study has tested
glutathione with
retinol or exfoliating acids, so add one new active at a time.

### Is a glutathione serum the same as oral or injected glutathione?

No. They are different routes with different studies. Our evidence is for glutathione applied to the skin, and
we make no
claim from oral or intravenous use.

### What are the side effects of a glutathione serum?

In the manufacturer's trial, all 30 women completed 10 weeks of twice-daily use, with no reactions attributed to
glutathione; one woman had mild redness over the whole face on days 2 and 3 that cleared by day 4. In the
independent
trial, no redness, swelling, stinging or itching was reported. The authors say more studies are needed on
long-term use,
and the trials covered skin types III to V. Patch test on a small area first, and stop using the serum if
stinging or
redness does not settle.
