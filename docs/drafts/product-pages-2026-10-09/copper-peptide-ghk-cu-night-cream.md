Use in the evening as the final step in your routine. Warm a pea-sized amount (a little more if your skin is
dry) and massage over the face and neck, after your serum. Its rich texture suits overnight wear.

If you also use pure L-ascorbic acid vitamin C, strong acids (AHA or BHA) or a high-strength retinol, apply
those at a different time of day or on another evening.

## Clinical research

**GHK-Cu, the copper peptide in this cream, has a double-blind trial and a 12-week face-cream study behind it.**

In a randomised, double-blind, split-face trial of a GHK-Cu serum:

- 40 women aged 40 to 65 used it twice a day for 8 weeks (39 were analysed)
- Wrinkle volume and depth fell further with the GHK-Cu serum than with the plain serum base, measured by 3D
  skin imaging of a crow's-feet wrinkle
- Change was measured at week 4 and had grown by week 8
- It was well tolerated by 39 of the 40 women; one had a minor reaction, stopped, and it settled without
  treatment

Also on record:

- A copper-peptide face cream was studied for 12 weeks in 71 women and presented at the American Academy of
  Dermatology meeting in 2002. The reported results were improved firmness, clarity and appearance, with
  fewer fine lines. It is a conference abstract and was not published in full
- In a one-month skin-biopsy study of 20 volunteers, more people using a copper-peptide cream showed an
  increase in procollagen (the precursor of collagen) than with vitamin C, melatonin or tretinoin creams (10
  people per cream, thigh skin, no statistical test between the creams)
- An independent 2026 systematic review of 20 studies, two of them randomised trials, concluded that GHK-Cu
  significantly reduced wrinkle volume and depth compared with controls. It also noted a limited number of
  well-designed clinical trials

Sources: Badenhorst et al. 2016, J Aging Sci 4(3):166 (the GHK-Cu serum used a lipid nano-carrier, and the
plain serum base carried neither the peptide nor the carrier, so the difference covers both; funded by the
test serum's maker, with independent statistics); Leyden et al. 2002, American Academy of Dermatology 60th
Annual Meeting, abstract P68 (as reported in published reviews; the abstract itself is unread); Abdulghani et
al. 1998, Disease Management and Clinical Outcomes; Mokhtar et al. 2026, Aesthetic Surgery Journal. Results
describe the ingredient as studied, not this product; the full figures are on the research page.

## FAQ

### What does GHK-Cu do for skin?

GHK-Cu is a copper peptide your own body makes, first isolated from human plasma in 1973. In a double-blind
trial, a GHK-Cu serum reduced crow's-feet wrinkle volume and depth further than the plain serum base after
8 weeks, and an independent 2026 review reached the same conclusion on wrinkle volume and depth. The human
evidence is still a handful of trials. In your routine, this cream is for firmer-looking, smoother-looking
skin.

### What should you not mix copper peptides with?

No combination has been tested in a trial. Copper peptides are not acids or exfoliants, so they layer easily
with other peptides, hyaluronic acid and moisturisers. If you separately use pure L-ascorbic acid vitamin C,
strong acids (AHA or BHA) or a high-strength retinol, apply those at a different time of day or on another
evening. Introduce one new product at a time.

### How often should you use copper peptides on your face?

Use this cream once a day, in the evening, as the last step after your serum. The trial applied a GHK-Cu
serum twice a day and measured change at weeks 4 and 8, so judge any copper peptide over at least 8 weeks of
regular use. Pairing it with the Renewal Serum morning and evening follows the same twice-daily pattern.

### Which copper peptide cream is right for me, night or day?

The Night Cream is richer and made for the evening, for normal-to-dry and mature skin. The Day Gel-Cream is
lighter, sits under SPF and makeup, and suits normal, combination and oily skin; it also contains vitamin C.
Both carry 2% GHK-Cu. The Night Cream does not list vitamin C.

### Can I use this GHK-Cu cream with the Copper Peptide serum?

Yes. Apply the Copper Peptide (GHK-Cu) 2% Renewal Serum first, then seal with this cream. If you would rather
keep vitamin C out of your evening, use the cream after a serum of your choice.

### What are the downsides of copper peptides, and should I patch test?

In the 8-week controlled trial, 39 of 40 women tolerated a GHK-Cu serum well, with none of the peeling,
dryness or redness seen with retinoids. One woman had a minor reaction on both sides of her face, stopped,
and it settled without treatment. That trial did not state its concentration, and our 2% products have no
tolerance study of their own, so patch test first: apply a small amount behind the ear for 24 to 48 hours.
Stop using it if stinging or redness does not settle, and see a pharmacist or dermatologist if a reaction
persists. Keep it away from the eyes and broken skin. It has not been tested in pregnancy or while
breastfeeding, so ask your doctor or pharmacist first.
