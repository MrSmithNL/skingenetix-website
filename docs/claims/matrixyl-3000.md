# Claims register: Matrixyl® 3000 (palmitoyl tripeptide-1 + palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7), with palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 (Matrixyl®)

**Built:** 2026-09-22 · **Scope:** Matrixyl 3000 Firming Serum (30 ml), Matrixyl 3000 Pro-Collagen Firming Cream (50 ml), the Full Matrixyl 3000 Ritual bundle, and `/pages/matrixyl-3000-research`.
**Standing rule:** use the strongest claim the best evidence supports. It has to be true, sourced, and within EU Regulation 655/2013. That regulation sets six common criteria: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness and informed decision-making. Every claim here must also read as cosmetic (how skin looks and feels), not medicinal.
**Verification standard:** I checked every number below on 2026-09-22 against a primary text: a full paper, an abstract, a patent, or the manufacturer's own brochure. Where only a secondary source was available, the row says so and the grade is lower.

**Grades:** **A** = controlled human study with instrument measurement · **B** = uncontrolled or self-assessed human data, or **manufacturer in-vivo data** · **C** = ex vivo (excised human skin) · **D** = in vitro or animal.

**Three consequences drive most of this register:**

1. **The cream's Clinical Research block says "The hero peptide (Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4)…", but the cream does not contain that peptide.** Under the truthfulness criterion that is the most serious fault on these pages. Remove it from the cream now.
2. **"10% MATRIXYL" is ambiguous and has to be confirmed against the formula sheet before any copy uses it.** Matrixyl® and Matrixyl® 3000 are *supplier solutions*. Sederma's INCI for Matrixyl 3000 is "Glycerin – Aqua – Butylene Glycol – Carbomer – Polysorbate 20 – Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 – Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7", and the peptides are present only at parts-per-million. So "10%" means 10% of the trade solution, **not 10% peptide**. We need to know (a) which solution it refers to (Matrixyl 3000, Matrixyl, or the two combined) and (b) the split between them. **If it is ≥3% Matrixyl 3000, it is a strong asset:** Sederma recommends 3% and ran its clinical studies at 3%. Claims 1–3 below depend on this.
3. **Every Matrixyl 3000 human study is manufacturer data.** No independent, controlled trial of Matrixyl 3000 on its own has been published (PubMed and Europe PMC searched 2026-09-22). The only independent RCT in this register tests **palmitoyl pentapeptide-4**, and **Procter & Gamble** ran it, so it is not independent either (see §2).

**The EU rule that governs how we attach ingredient data to our products.** The EC Technical Document on Cosmetic Claims (2017), Annex I, evidential support, says: *"A claim extrapolating (explicitly or implicitly) ingredient properties to the finished product should be supported by adequate and verifiable evidence, such as by demonstrating the presence of the ingredient at an effective concentration."* It also says: *"The presentation of results from in vitro or in silico studies should not suggest a result in vivo."* Source: https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/24847

## 1. Headline claims, ready to use (ranked)

### Claim 1 (hero). Deep wrinkles visibly reduced in two months (manufacturer clinical study)
**Claim:** *"In a 2-month clinical study, Matrixyl® 3000 reduced the area of deep wrinkles by 39% and main-wrinkle depth by 20%. The placebo side showed no significant change."*
Short form: *"−39% deep-wrinkle area in 2 months (clinical study on Matrixyl® 3000)."*

- **Fact, read from Sederma's brochure (v.131030, "Claim substantiation 10-2013 v.4"):** 23 women aged 42–67. A cream containing **3% Matrixyl 3000** was applied twice daily to one half of the face and a placebo to the other half, for 2 months. Wrinkles were measured by profilometry, cutometry and photography. The brochure's results are changes from day 0 (T0):

| Measure | Matrixyl 3000 side | Placebo side |
|---|---|---|
| Surface occupied by deep wrinkles | **−39.4%** (p<0.01 vs T0) | 4.3%, not significant |
| Main-wrinkle density | **−32.9%** (p<0.01) | −9.9%, ns |
| Main-wrinkle average depth | **−19.9%** (p<0.01) | −3.2%, ns |
| Main-wrinkle average volume | **−23.3%** (p<0.01) | −8.7% (p<0.05) |
| Roughness | **−16.0%** (p<0.01) | −1.4%, ns |
| "Complexity" (Sederma calls it a lifting effect) | **−16.2%** (p<0.01) | 4.2%, ns |
| Elasticity | **+5.5%** (p<0.05) | 4.1%, ns |
| Skin tone | **+15.5%** (p<0.01) | 6.5%, ns |

- **Source:** Sederma, *Matrixyl® 3000* brochure, v.131030 (2013). The copy I read is hosted by a distributor: https://irp-cdn.multiscreensite.com/2d058be6/files/uploaded/POD-Matrixyl%203000-%20Brochure%20%281%29%20%281%29.pdf. (The 28-volunteer study in claim 3 is the one in Sederma's patent WO2012164488A2; this 23-volunteer panel appears only in the brochure.)
- **Grade: B.** It is manufacturer data, not peer-reviewed. The significance tests compare each side with its own baseline; **the brochure reports no direct active-versus-placebo test.** The sample is small.
- **Attach to:** the serum, **only once the formula sheet confirms ≥3% Matrixyl 3000**. The cream qualifies only once its Matrixyl 3000 level is known to be ≥3%. Before that, use it on the hub page only.
- **Conditions:** (a) say "clinical study on Matrixyl® 3000", never "our serum was tested"; (b) write "the placebo side showed no significant change", **not** "vs placebo"; (c) keep the word "area" in −39%, because "39% fewer wrinkles" would be false; (d) EU: "wrinkles look reduced" and "visibly smoother" are cosmetic wording, and that is fine here.

### Claim 2. Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 beat placebo in a 12-week, 93-woman double-blind trial (serum only)
**Claim:** *"Our serum also contains palmitoyl pentapeptide-4. In a 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 93 women, it significantly reduced the look of wrinkles and fine lines compared with placebo, measured by instrument and by expert graders."*

- **Fact (abstract):** 93 Caucasian women aged 35–55. 12 weeks. Double-blind, placebo-controlled, split-face, left-right randomised. The test was a moisturiser containing **3 ppm pal-KTTKS** against the same moisturiser without it. The result was *"significant improvement vs. placebo control for reduction in wrinkles/fine lines by both quantitative technical and expert grader image analysis"*. Subjects' self-assessments also showed significant improvement. The peptide was *"well tolerated by the skin"*. The CIR safety review summarises the full text: the effect *"was described as small, but was significant at weeks 8 and 12"*.
- **Source:** Robinson LR, Fitzgerald NC, Doughty DG, Dawes NC, Berge CA, Bissett DL. *Int J Cosmet Sci* 2005;27(3):155–160. PMID 18492182. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18492182/
- **Grade: A.** It is controlled, randomised and instrumental. **Conflict of interest: all six authors are from The Procter & Gamble Company** (Miami Valley Laboratories), which sells pal-KTTKS in Olay. **This is not an independent trial.** I read the abstract only; the full text is paywalled, so no effect size is available.
- **Attach to:** **serum only.** Never the cream (it has no pentapeptide-4), and never Matrixyl 3000 itself. **Conditions:** (a) name pentapeptide-4 as the subject; (b) drop the word "independent"; (c) no percentage, because none was published in anything I could read; (d) the tested level was 3 ppm, so check that the serum's pentapeptide-4 content is at least that. At normal supplier-solution use levels it almost certainly is, but confirm it.

### Claim 3. Firmer-looking skin, measured beneath the surface (manufacturer ultrasound study)
**Claim:** *"In a 2-month placebo-controlled study, ultrasound showed the upper skin layer 15% denser on the Matrixyl® 3000 side than on the placebo side, with the change already measurable after one month."*

- **Fact (Sederma brochure v.131030):** 28 women aged 51–72 (mean 59). A cream containing 3% Matrixyl 3000 was used twice daily for 2 months on one half of the face and on the forearm (inner and UV-exposed outer), against placebo. **Ultrasound of the subepidermal low-echogenicity band (SLEB)** measured density and thickness. **The SLEB was measured on the forearm.** After 2 months, density was **+15.2% vs placebo (inner forearm)** and **+15.1% vs placebo (outer)**, p<0.01. Thickness was −11% vs placebo (inner) and −14.4% vs placebo (outer), p<0.01. After 1 month (inner arm), thickness was −5.5% and density +7.8% vs placebo. On the **face**, confocal microscopy next to the outer eye corner showed a **+13.9% vs T0** improvement in the dermal fibre network (p<0.01; up to +54%; 71% of volunteers improved).
- **Also in patent WO2012164488A2 (Sederma), read at source:** https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2012164488A2/en. The patent gives the design as blind, randomised, placebo on the contralateral side, with Student t or Wilcoxon tests on paired series. It reports outer-forearm thickness −14.4% and density +15.1%, both p<0.01. Face fibre circumference was +11.1% at 1 month and +13.9% at 2 months vs T0, against placebo +4.5% and +0.7% (ns).
- **Grade: B.** It is manufacturer data. The active-versus-placebo comparison is reported here, unlike in claim 1.
- **Attach to:** same condition as claim 1 (≥3% confirmed). **Conditions:** (a) the density figure is **forearm** data, so do not put it beside a face photo or imply it was measured on the face; (b) keep it in study-report form. "Rebuilds the dermis" or "repairs collagen" would read as medicinal. "Firmer-looking" and "denser on ultrasound" are fine.

### Claim 4. Two peptides together work far better than either alone (lab data)
**Claim:** *"In lab tests on skin cells, the two Matrixyl® 3000 peptides together raised collagen I by 256%. That is nearly four times what the stronger peptide achieved on its own."*

- **Fact (patent Table 1, read at source):** normal human skin fibroblasts, 3-day contact, ELISA, n=3 per test. Results are % change vs solvent:
  - pal-GQPR (tetrapeptide-7) alone, 3.64 ppm: collagen I **+65%**
  - pal-GHK (tripeptide-1) alone, 7.3 ppm: collagen I **−9%**
  - **Combination, 7.3 + 3.64 ppm: collagen I +256%, fibronectin +147%, collagen IV +20%, hyaluronic acid +92%**
  - For comparison, pal-KTTKS (Matrixyl) at 8 ppm: collagen I +93%, collagen IV +22%.
- **Source:** Lintner K (Sederma SA). US 6,974,799 B2, *Compositions containing mixtures of tetrapeptides and tripeptides* (priority 2003-11). https://patents.google.com/patent/US6974799B2/en. The CIR 2012 review reports Sederma's related marketing figures for Matrixyl 3000 as fibronectin **+164%** and hyaluronic acid **+179%**; a secondary copy of Sederma's synopsis gives collagen I **+258%**. Those three figures come from a different presentation that I could not read at Sederma. **Use the patent numbers.**
- **Grade: D** (in vitro, manufacturer).
- **Attach to:** serum, cream and hub, as an ingredient claim. **Conditions:** (a) **"in lab tests on skin cells" must stay in the sentence**, because the EC document forbids in-vitro results that suggest an in-vivo result; (b) never "boosts collagen in your skin by 256%"; (c) collagen language reports the lab study and is not a product promise.

### Claim 5. Matrixyl 3000's two peptides are assessed safe in cosmetics by an independent expert panel
**Claim:** *"Palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7 are assessed as safe in cosmetics at current use levels by the independent Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel."*

- **Fact:** The CIR Expert Panel (March 2014 tentative, then final) *"concluded that these ingredients are safe in the present practices of use and concentration in cosmetics"*. It noted that *"the low use concentrations and negative safety test data reviewed obviate any concerns"* and that typical use is below 10 ppm. The data it reviewed included human repeated-insult patch tests on pal-tripeptide-1 trade materials (for example, MAXI-LIP, 52 subjects: "no clinically significant potential for skin irritation or sensitization"), and Sederma supplied HRIPT, irritation and Ames data on pal-tetrapeptide-7.
- **Source:** CIR final report: Johnson W Jr et al., *Safety Assessment of Tripeptide-1, Hexapeptide-12, Their Metal Salts and Fatty Acyl Derivatives, and Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7 as Used in Cosmetics*, *Int J Toxicol* 2018. DOI 10.1177/1091581818807863. Panel draft-final text read at https://www.cir-safety.org/sites/default/files/peptides.pdf.
- **Grade:** this is a safety assessment, so it does not carry an efficacy grade. It is a strong, independent trust signal.
- **Attach to:** serum, cream and hub. **Conditions:** it is an **ingredient** safety finding. It does not show that *our formula* is well tolerated (see §2, "Well tolerated"). Do not turn it into "clinically proven gentle".

### Claim 6. Results you can measure in 8 weeks
**Claim:** *"Measurable wrinkle results in 8 weeks: significant in the pentapeptide-4 trial from week 8, and in the Matrixyl® 3000 studies at 2 months."*
Plain-customer form, for "What to expect": *"Most studies of these peptides measured results at 8 weeks, and they kept building to 12."*

- **Fact:** Robinson 2005 was significant at weeks 8 and 12 (per CIR's summary of the full text). The Sederma Matrixyl 3000 studies ran for 2 months (claims 1 and 3), with changes measurable at 1 month on ultrasound.
- **Grade:** it inherits A (Robinson) and B (Sederma).
- **Attach to:** "What to expect" on both products. On the **cream**, drop the pentapeptide-4 half and say "the Matrixyl 3000 studies ran for 2 months". **This replaces** the current serum line *"smoother, more hydrated skin within days"*, which has no data (see §2).

### Claim 7 (conditional). Formulated above the level used in the clinical studies
**Claim (only if the formula sheet confirms it):** *"10% Matrixyl® 3000 complex, over three times the 3% level used in the manufacturer's clinical studies."*

- **Fact:** Sederma's recommended use level is **3%**, and every Sederma in-vivo study I could read used **3%** (brochure v.131030; CIR 2012 summary of the 24- and 25-volunteer panels).
- **Grade:** this is a fact about our product plus a B-grade reference.
- **Conditions:** (a) the **formula sheet must confirm** that 10% is Matrixyl 3000 solution specifically, not Matrixyl + Matrixyl 3000 combined; (b) add "(as supplied solution)" or "complex", because otherwise readers take it as 10% peptide; (c) **do not say or imply "3× stronger results".** No data shows that more than 3% works better, so the claim is only that we exceed the tested level. That is also the fact that makes claims 1 and 3 legitimate to attach to the serum under the EC "effective concentration" test.

## 4. Claims to AVOID

| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| "+117% collagen I / +327% collagen IV" (any product, any ingredient) | Untraceable; contradicted for Matrixyl 3000 by Sederma's own patent (collagen IV +20%). See §2 #1. |
| "Independent clinical trial" / "independent RCT" | No independent controlled trial of Matrixyl 3000 exists. The pentapeptide-4 RCT was run by P&G. Badenhorst was funded by a GHK-Cu maker. Watson was funded by Boots. |
| Any pentapeptide-4 claim on the **cream** | The cream does not contain it. |
| "Boosts collagen in your skin", "rebuilds collagen", "repairs the dermis", "stimulates collagen production" as a **product promise** | The only collagen data are in vitro (the EC document forbids implying in vivo) or ultrasound *density*, which is not collagen. As a product action it also risks a medicinal reading. Allowed: "in lab tests on skin cells, … raised collagen I". |
| "Matrixyl 3000 Collagen Boosting Serum" (hub CTA) and "Firming **Repair Treatment**" (pack name) | "Collagen boosting" states a physiological action. "Repair Treatment" leans medicinal. Prefer "Firming Serum" / "Firming Treatment". The pack name needs a separate decision because it is printed artwork. |
| "Clinically proven" (unqualified) | The only Matrixyl 3000 human data are manufacturer data. "Clinically studied ingredient" and "tested in a 2-month clinical study (manufacturer data)" are accurate. |
| "Slows ageing by 1.8 years" / "up to 5.5 years younger skin" | Sederma converts ultrasound change into "age gain" years. It is a modelled figure, on forearm skin, and consumers read it as literal rejuvenation. It is misleading under the honesty and truthfulness criteria. |
| "Up to 54% …", "up to 44/45% …" | "Up to" figures are the single best volunteer. The EC document and ordinary honesty require the mean to lead. |
| "39% fewer wrinkles" | The figure is the *surface area occupied by deep wrinkles*, not a count. |
| "Better than retinol" / "a retinol alternative with the same results" | No head-to-head study. Schagen's line "comparable to retinoic acid with regards to its activity" is unsourced, and is about pal-GHK in vitro. |
| "Suitable for sensitive skin", "hypoallergenic", "dermatologist tested", "no photosensitivity" | No product-level data. "Hypoallergenic" requires the Annex IV evidence standard. |
| "Penetrates deep into the dermis" | Ex vivo OrbiSIMS showed pal-GHK delivered *within the stratum corneum*, reaching baseline at the SC–epidermis boundary (Starr 2022). CIR 2023 draft: pal-pentapeptide-4 did not permeate full-thickness hairless-mouse skin. |
| "Outperforms classic Matrixyl" | Sederma's own head-to-head (CIR 2012 summary): **not significantly different**. |
| Badenhorst's "−20% wrinkle volume" for a Matrixyl 3000 product | It is a competitor product full of other actives, the vehicle alone gave −15%, and in that trial the GHK-Cu serum beat it. Unhelpful and not attributable. |
| Watson 2009 (No7) results as Matrixyl 3000 evidence | Multi-ingredient (retinyl palmitate included). The 6-month between-group difference was not significant. |
| "10% Matrixyl" without "complex" / "as supplied" | Readers take it as 10% peptide. See §0 and claim 7. |
| "Free from parabens" | EC Technical Document Annex III: "should not be accepted". |

## 5. Safety and tolerability, as reported

- **CIR Expert Panel (independent), final 2014/2018:** tripeptide-1 (and its fatty-acyl derivatives, including palmitoyl tripeptide-1) and palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7 are **"safe in the present practices of use and concentration in cosmetics"**. Typical use is below 10 ppm. Human data reviewed include an HRIPT of MAXI-LIP (1,000 ppm pal-GHK, undiluted, 52 completers): "barely perceptible (+) to moderate (2) reaction during induction and/or challenge… no clinically significant potential for skin irritation or sensitization". Sederma (Dr Lintner) supplied HRIPT, irritation, ocular and Ames data on pal-tetrapeptide-7 to the panel. Rigin™ (500 ppm pal-tetrapeptide-7) was "slightly irritating" in the HET-CAM *eye* assay.
- **CIR 2012 SLR:** "Skin irritation and sensitization studies on palmitoyl peptides were not found in the published literature." The industry data above was submitted later.
- **Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4:** Robinson 2005, 3 ppm, 93 women, 12 weeks: "well tolerated by the skin". Aruan 2023: no complaints in the PPP-4 arm (n=7). In the same trial the *placebo* arm reported itching (29%) and burning (29%), which is a reminder that vehicles cause reactions too. CIR's pentapeptide-4 report was a **draft (August 2023)** when I read it, listing an HRIPT on a 0.12% pal-pentapeptide-4 formulation (51 subjects) among the submitted data. Check whether the final conclusion has been published before citing it.
- **Badenhorst 2016:** of 40 subjects, one had "minor unwanted skin reactions" on both sides (GHK-Cu serum and the Matrixyl 3000 product) and stopped. The reaction resolved without treatment.
- **Yang 2024 (multi-ingredient eye cream):** "no adverse reactions" observed by dermatologists over 12 weeks (uncontrolled).
- **Sederma:** the brochure reports no tolerance data. Patent WO2012164488A2 (28 volunteers, 3%, 2 months) says the study "was conducted under medical supervision and demonstrated that product tolerability by the volunteers was excellent". That is manufacturer data, with no numbers given.
- **Our products:** **no product-level tolerance study on record.** That is why "Well tolerated" has to be reworded (§2 #5).

## 6. Gaps

1. **Formula sheet (blocking claims 1, 3 and 7).** Confirm what "10% MATRIXYL" on the serum pack means: Matrixyl 3000 solution, Matrixyl (pentapeptide-4) solution, or both combined, and at what split. Get the cream's Matrixyl 3000 level. Without that, the Sederma in-vivo data can only sit on the hub page, not on the products.
2. **Cream INCI oddities, to be checked against the carton.** The cream's full list on `/pages/ingredients` ends in **Acetyl Hexapeptide-8** (unmentioned in its copy) and lists no elastin, although the FAQ says the cream contains collagen and elastin. The **PDRN + Collagen Night Cream's** list ends "…Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1, Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7" and contains the squalane and allantoin that the Matrixyl cream names as key actives. **Possible swapped INCI lists between the two creams; verify against the carton artwork.**
3. **"Vegan" contradiction:** `/pages/ingredients` says every formula is vegan; the cream's FAQ says it is not (§2 #19).
4. **No product-level tolerance test (HRIPT / in-use).** Commissioning one (typically 50+ subjects) would unlock "well tolerated" and possibly "suitable for sensitive skin" as product claims.
5. **No independent controlled trial of Matrixyl 3000 alone exists** (PubMed and Europe PMC, 2026-09-22). This is a real gap in the science, and the hub page should say so honestly (the copper hub now does the same).
6. **Robinson 2005 full text not read** (paywalled). No effect size is available; the "small, significant at weeks 8 and 12" wording comes from CIR's summary of the full text.
7. **Sederma primary documents.** I read the 2013 brochure through a distributor's upload. The older 24/25-volunteer data are known only through CIR and an archived third-party page. Asking our supplier for the current Croda/Sederma technical dossier would confirm the figures and may add newer studies (for example, men's panels).
8. **Trademark use.** Matrixyl® and Matrixyl® 3000 are Sederma (Croda) trademarks. Confirm with the supplier that our product names and the ® usage are permitted. Sederma runs a co-branding programme ("matrixylinside.com" is printed on the 2013 brochure).
9. **Gorouhi & Maibach 2009** (the only systematic review of controlled topical-peptide studies): full text not accessed. It may hold a critical appraisal worth quoting on the hub page.
10. **Newer trials, 2018–2026:** none found for Matrixyl 3000 alone. The only newer items are multi-ingredient (Yang 2024, Cayce 2026 10-peptide serum, open-label) and cannot be attributed to the peptides.
