# Claims register: Matrixyl® 3000 (palmitoyl tripeptide-1 + palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7), with palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 (Matrixyl®)

**Built:** 2026-09-22 · **Scope:** Matrixyl 3000 Firming Serum (30 ml), Matrixyl 3000 Pro-Collagen Firming Cream (50 ml), the Full Matrixyl 3000 Ritual bundle, and `/pages/matrixyl-3000-research`.
**Standing rule:** use the strongest claim the best evidence supports. It has to be true, sourced, and within EU Regulation 655/2013. That regulation sets six
common criteria: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness and informed decision-making. Every claim here must also read as
cosmetic (how skin looks and feels), not medicinal.
**Verification standard:** I checked every number below on 2026-09-22 against a primary text: a full paper, an abstract, a patent, or the manufacturer's own brochure. Where only a secondary source was available, the row says so and the grade is lower.

**Grades:** **A** = controlled human study with instrument measurement · **B** = uncontrolled or self-assessed human data, or **manufacturer in-vivo data** · **C** = ex vivo (excised human skin) · **D** = in vitro or animal.

**Three consequences drive most of this register:**

1. **The cream's Clinical Research block says "The hero peptide (Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4)…", but the cream does not contain that peptide.** Under the truthfulness criterion that is the most serious fault on these pages. Remove it from the cream now.
2. **"10% MATRIXYL" is ambiguous and has to be confirmed against the formula sheet before any copy uses it.** Matrixyl® and Matrixyl® 3000 are _supplier
   solutions_. Sederma's INCI for Matrixyl 3000 is "Glycerin – Aqua – Butylene Glycol – Carbomer – Polysorbate 20 – Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 – Palmitoyl
   Tetrapeptide-7", and the peptides are present only at parts-per-million. So "10%" means 10% of the trade solution, **not 10% peptide**. We need to know (a)
   which solution it refers to (Matrixyl 3000, Matrixyl, or the two combined) and (b) the split between them. **If it is ≥3% Matrixyl 3000, it is a strong
   asset:** Sederma recommends 3% and ran its clinical studies at 3%. Claims 1–3 below depend on this.
3. **Every Matrixyl 3000 human study is manufacturer data.** No independent, controlled trial of Matrixyl 3000 on its own has been published (PubMed and
   Europe PMC searched 2026-09-22). The only independent RCT in this register tests **palmitoyl pentapeptide-4**, and **Procter & Gamble** ran it, so it is
   not independent either (see §2).

**The EU rule that governs how we attach ingredient data to our products.** The EC Technical Document on Cosmetic Claims (2017), Annex I, evidential support,
says: _"A claim extrapolating (explicitly or implicitly) ingredient properties to the finished product should be supported by adequate and verifiable
evidence, such as by demonstrating the presence of the ingredient at an effective concentration."_ It also says: _"The presentation of results from in vitro
or in silico studies should not suggest a result in vivo."_ Source: https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/24847

## 1. Headline claims, ready to use (ranked)

### Claim 1 (hero). Deep wrinkles visibly reduced in two months (manufacturer clinical study)

**Claim:** _"In a 2-month clinical study, Matrixyl® 3000 reduced the area of deep wrinkles by 39% and main-wrinkle depth by 20%. The placebo side showed no significant change."_
Short form: _"−39% deep-wrinkle area in 2 months (clinical study on Matrixyl® 3000)."_

- **Fact, read from Sederma's brochure (v.131030, "Claim substantiation 10-2013 v.4"):** 23 women aged 42–67. A cream containing **3% Matrixyl 3000** was
  applied twice daily to one half of the face and a placebo to the other half, for 2 months. Wrinkles were measured by profilometry, cutometry and
  photography. The brochure's results are changes from day 0 (T0):

| Measure                                          | Matrixyl 3000 side        | Placebo side          |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Surface occupied by deep wrinkles                | **−39.4%** (p<0.01 vs T0) | 4.3%, not significant |
| Main-wrinkle density                             | **−32.9%** (p<0.01)       | −9.9%, ns             |
| Main-wrinkle average depth                       | **−19.9%** (p<0.01)       | −3.2%, ns             |
| Main-wrinkle average volume                      | **−23.3%** (p<0.01)       | −8.7% (p<0.05)        |
| Roughness                                        | **−16.0%** (p<0.01)       | −1.4%, ns             |
| "Complexity" (Sederma calls it a lifting effect) | **−16.2%** (p<0.01)       | 4.2%, ns              |
| Elasticity                                       | **+5.5%** (p<0.05)        | 4.1%, ns              |
| Skin tone                                        | **+15.5%** (p<0.01)       | 6.5%, ns              |

- **Source:** Sederma, _Matrixyl® 3000_ brochure, v.131030 (2013). The copy I read is hosted by a distributor:
  https://irp-cdn.multiscreensite.com/2d058be6/files/uploaded/POD-Matrixyl%203000-%20Brochure%20%281%29%20%281%29.pdf. (The 28-volunteer study in claim 3 is
  the one in Sederma's patent WO2012164488A2; this 23-volunteer panel appears only in the brochure.)
- **Grade: B.** It is manufacturer data, not peer-reviewed. The significance tests compare each side with its own baseline; **the brochure reports no direct active-versus-placebo test.** The sample is small.
- **Attach to:** the serum, **only once the formula sheet confirms ≥3% Matrixyl 3000**. The cream qualifies only once its Matrixyl 3000 level is known to be ≥3%. Before that, use it on the hub page only.
- **Conditions:** (a) say "clinical study on Matrixyl® 3000", never "our serum was tested"; (b) write "the placebo side showed no significant change", **not**
  "vs placebo"; (c) keep the word "area" in −39%, because "39% fewer wrinkles" would be false; (d) EU: "wrinkles look reduced" and "visibly smoother" are
  cosmetic wording, and that is fine here.

### Claim 2. Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 beat placebo in a 12-week, 93-woman double-blind trial (serum only)

**Claim:** _"Our serum also contains palmitoyl pentapeptide-4. In a 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 93 women, it significantly reduced the look of wrinkles and fine lines compared with placebo, measured by instrument and by expert graders."_

- **Fact (abstract):** 93 Caucasian women aged 35–55. 12 weeks. Double-blind, placebo-controlled, split-face, left-right randomised. The test was a
  moisturiser containing **3 ppm pal-KTTKS** against the same moisturiser without it. The result was _"significant improvement vs. placebo control for
  reduction in wrinkles/fine lines by both quantitative technical and expert grader image analysis"_. Subjects' self-assessments also showed significant
  improvement. The peptide was _"well tolerated by the skin"_. The CIR safety review summarises the full text: the effect _"was described as small, but was
  significant at weeks 8 and 12"_.
- **Source:** Robinson LR, Fitzgerald NC, Doughty DG, Dawes NC, Berge CA, Bissett DL. _Int J Cosmet Sci_ 2005;27(3):155–160. PMID 18492182. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18492182/
- **Grade: A.** It is controlled, randomised and instrumental. **Conflict of interest: all six authors are from The Procter & Gamble Company** (Miami Valley
  Laboratories), which sells pal-KTTKS in Olay. **This is not an independent trial.** I read the abstract only; the full text is paywalled, so no effect size
  is available.
- **Attach to:** **serum only.** Never the cream (it has no pentapeptide-4), and never Matrixyl 3000 itself. **Conditions:** (a) name pentapeptide-4 as the
  subject; (b) drop the word "independent"; (c) no percentage, because none was published in anything I could read; (d) the tested level was 3 ppm, so check
  that the serum's pentapeptide-4 content is at least that. At normal supplier-solution use levels it almost certainly is, but confirm it.

### Claim 3. Firmer-looking skin, measured beneath the surface (manufacturer ultrasound study)

**Claim:** _"In a 2-month placebo-controlled study, ultrasound showed the upper skin layer 15% denser on the Matrixyl® 3000 side than on the placebo side, with the change already measurable after one month."_

- **Fact (Sederma brochure v.131030):** 28 women aged 51–72 (mean 59). A cream containing 3% Matrixyl 3000 was used twice daily for 2 months on one half of
  the face and on the forearm (inner and UV-exposed outer), against placebo. **Ultrasound of the subepidermal low-echogenicity band (SLEB)** measured density
  and thickness. **The SLEB was measured on the forearm.** After 2 months, density was **+15.2% vs placebo (inner forearm)** and **+15.1% vs placebo
  (outer)**, p<0.01. Thickness was −11% vs placebo (inner) and −14.4% vs placebo (outer), p<0.01. After 1 month (inner arm), thickness was −5.5% and density
  +7.8% vs placebo. On the **face**, confocal microscopy next to the outer eye corner showed a **+13.9% vs T0** improvement in the dermal fibre network
  (p<0.01; up to +54%; 71% of volunteers improved).
- **Also in patent WO2012164488A2 (Sederma), read at source:** https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2012164488A2/en. The patent gives the design as blind,
  randomised, placebo on the contralateral side, with Student t or Wilcoxon tests on paired series. It reports outer-forearm thickness −14.4% and density
  +15.1%, both p<0.01. Face fibre circumference was +11.1% at 1 month and +13.9% at 2 months vs T0, against placebo +4.5% and +0.7% (ns).
- **Grade: B.** It is manufacturer data. The active-versus-placebo comparison is reported here, unlike in claim 1.
- **Attach to:** same condition as claim 1 (≥3% confirmed). **Conditions:** (a) the density figure is **forearm** data, so do not put it beside a face photo
  or imply it was measured on the face; (b) keep it in study-report form. "Rebuilds the dermis" or "repairs collagen" would read as medicinal.
  "Firmer-looking" and "denser on ultrasound" are fine.

### Claim 4. Two peptides together work far better than either alone (lab data)

**Claim:** _"In lab tests on skin cells, the two Matrixyl® 3000 peptides together raised collagen I by 256%. That is nearly four times what the stronger peptide achieved on its own."_

- **Fact (patent Table 1, read at source):** normal human skin fibroblasts, 3-day contact, ELISA, n=3 per test. Results are % change vs solvent:
  - pal-GQPR (tetrapeptide-7) alone, 3.64 ppm: collagen I **+65%**
  - pal-GHK (tripeptide-1) alone, 7.3 ppm: collagen I **−9%**
  - **Combination, 7.3 + 3.64 ppm: collagen I +256%, fibronectin +147%, collagen IV +20%, hyaluronic acid +92%**
  - For comparison, pal-KTTKS (Matrixyl) at 8 ppm: collagen I +93%, collagen IV +22%.
- **Source:** Lintner K (Sederma SA). US 6,974,799 B2, _Compositions containing mixtures of tetrapeptides and tripeptides_ (priority 2003-11).
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US6974799B2/en. The CIR 2012 review reports Sederma's related marketing figures for Matrixyl 3000 as fibronectin **+164%**
  and hyaluronic acid **+179%**; a secondary copy of Sederma's synopsis gives collagen I **+258%**. Those three figures come from a different presentation
  that I could not read at Sederma. **Use the patent numbers.**
- **Grade: D** (in vitro, manufacturer).
- **Attach to:** serum, cream and hub, as an ingredient claim. **Conditions:** (a) **"in lab tests on skin cells" must stay in the sentence**, because the EC
  document forbids in-vitro results that suggest an in-vivo result; (b) never "boosts collagen in your skin by 256%"; (c) collagen language reports the lab
  study and is not a product promise.

### Claim 5. Matrixyl 3000's two peptides are assessed safe in cosmetics by an independent expert panel

**Claim:** _"Palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7 are assessed as safe in cosmetics at current use levels by the independent Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel."_

- **Fact:** The CIR Expert Panel (March 2014 tentative, then final) _"concluded that these ingredients are safe in the present practices of use and
  concentration in cosmetics"_. It noted that _"the low use concentrations and negative safety test data reviewed obviate any concerns"_ and that typical use
  is below 10 ppm. The data it reviewed included human repeated-insult patch tests on pal-tripeptide-1 trade materials (for example, MAXI-LIP, 52 subjects:
  "no clinically significant potential for skin irritation or sensitization"), and Sederma supplied HRIPT, irritation and Ames data on pal-tetrapeptide-7.
- **Source:** CIR final report: Johnson W Jr et al., _Safety Assessment of Tripeptide-1, Hexapeptide-12, Their Metal Salts and Fatty Acyl Derivatives, and
  Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7 as Used in Cosmetics_, _Int J Toxicol_ 2018. DOI 10.1177/1091581818807863. Panel draft-final text read at
  https://www.cir-safety.org/sites/default/files/peptides.pdf.
- **Grade:** this is a safety assessment, so it does not carry an efficacy grade. It is a strong, independent trust signal.
- **Attach to:** serum, cream and hub. **Conditions:** it is an **ingredient** safety finding. It does not show that _our formula_ is well tolerated (see §2, "Well tolerated"). Do not turn it into "clinically proven gentle".

### Claim 6. Results you can measure in 8 weeks

**Claim:** _"Measurable wrinkle results in 8 weeks: significant in the pentapeptide-4 trial from week 8, and in the Matrixyl® 3000 studies at 2 months."_
Plain-customer form, for "What to expect": _"Most studies of these peptides measured results at 8 weeks, and they kept building to 12."_

- **Fact:** Robinson 2005 was significant at weeks 8 and 12 (per CIR's summary of the full text). The Sederma Matrixyl 3000 studies ran for 2 months (claims 1 and 3), with changes measurable at 1 month on ultrasound.
- **Grade:** it inherits A (Robinson) and B (Sederma).
- **Attach to:** "What to expect" on both products. On the **cream**, drop the pentapeptide-4 half and say "the Matrixyl 3000 studies ran for 2 months". **This replaces** the current serum line _"smoother, more hydrated skin within days"_, which has no data (see §2).

### Claim 7 (conditional). Formulated above the level used in the clinical studies

**Claim (only if the formula sheet confirms it):** _"10% Matrixyl® 3000 complex, over three times the 3% level used in the manufacturer's clinical studies."_

- **Fact:** Sederma's recommended use level is **3%**, and every Sederma in-vivo study I could read used **3%** (brochure v.131030; CIR 2012 summary of the 24- and 25-volunteer panels).
- **Grade:** this is a fact about our product plus a B-grade reference.
- **Conditions:** (a) the **formula sheet must confirm** that 10% is Matrixyl 3000 solution specifically, not Matrixyl + Matrixyl 3000 combined; (b) add "(as
  supplied solution)" or "complex", because otherwise readers take it as 10% peptide; (c) **do not say or imply "3× stronger results".** No data shows that
  more than 3% works better, so the claim is only that we exceed the tested level. That is also the fact that makes claims 1 and 3 legitimate to attach to the
  serum under the EC "effective concentration" test.

## 5. Safety and tolerability, as reported

- **CIR Expert Panel (independent), final 2014/2018:** tripeptide-1 (and its fatty-acyl derivatives, including palmitoyl tripeptide-1) and palmitoyl
  tetrapeptide-7 are **"safe in the present practices of use and concentration in cosmetics"**. Typical use is below 10 ppm. Human data reviewed include an
  HRIPT of MAXI-LIP (1,000 ppm pal-GHK, undiluted, 52 completers): "barely perceptible (+) to moderate (2) reaction during induction and/or challenge… no
  clinically significant potential for skin irritation or sensitization". Sederma (Dr Lintner) supplied HRIPT, irritation, ocular and Ames data on
  pal-tetrapeptide-7 to the panel. Rigin™ (500 ppm pal-tetrapeptide-7) was "slightly irritating" in the HET-CAM _eye_ assay.
- **CIR 2012 SLR:** "Skin irritation and sensitization studies on palmitoyl peptides were not found in the published literature." The industry data above was submitted later.
- **Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4:** Robinson 2005, 3 ppm, 93 women, 12 weeks: "well tolerated by the skin". Aruan 2023: no complaints in the PPP-4 arm (n=7). In
  the same trial the _placebo_ arm reported itching (29%) and burning (29%), which is a reminder that vehicles cause reactions too. CIR's pentapeptide-4
  report was a **draft (August 2023)** when I read it, listing an HRIPT on a 0.12% pal-pentapeptide-4 formulation (51 subjects) among the submitted data.
  Check whether the final conclusion has been published before citing it.
- **Badenhorst 2016:** of 40 subjects, one had "minor unwanted skin reactions" on both sides (GHK-Cu serum and the Matrixyl 3000 product) and stopped. The reaction resolved without treatment.
- **Yang 2024 (multi-ingredient eye cream):** "no adverse reactions" observed by dermatologists over 12 weeks (uncontrolled).
- **Sederma:** the brochure reports no tolerance data. Patent WO2012164488A2 (28 volunteers, 3%, 2 months) says the study "was conducted under medical supervision and demonstrated that product tolerability by the volunteers was excellent". That is manufacturer data, with no numbers given.
- **Our products:** **no product-level tolerance study on record.** That is why "Well tolerated" has to be reworded (§2 #5).

## 6. Gaps

1. **Formula sheet (blocking claims 1, 3 and 7).** Confirm what "10% MATRIXYL" on the serum pack means: Matrixyl 3000 solution, Matrixyl (pentapeptide-4)
   solution, or both combined, and at what split. Get the cream's Matrixyl 3000 level. Without that, the Sederma in-vivo data can only sit on the hub page,
   not on the products.
2. **Cream INCI oddities, to be checked against the carton.** The cream's full list on `/pages/ingredients` ends in **Acetyl Hexapeptide-8** (unmentioned in
   its copy) and lists no elastin, although the FAQ says the cream contains collagen and elastin. The **PDRN + Collagen Night Cream's** list ends "…Palmitoyl
   Tripeptide-1, Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7" and contains the squalane and allantoin that the Matrixyl cream names as key actives. **Possible swapped INCI lists
   between the two creams; verify against the carton artwork.**
3. **"Vegan" contradiction:** `/pages/ingredients` says every formula is vegan; the cream's FAQ says it is not (§2 #19).
4. **No product-level tolerance test (HRIPT / in-use).** Commissioning one (typically 50+ subjects) would unlock "well tolerated" and possibly "suitable for sensitive skin" as product claims.
5. **No independent controlled trial of Matrixyl 3000 alone exists** (PubMed and Europe PMC, 2026-09-22). This is a real gap in the science, and the hub page should say so honestly (the copper hub now does the same).
6. **Robinson 2005 full text not read** (paywalled). No effect size is available; the "small, significant at weeks 8 and 12" wording comes from CIR's summary of the full text.
7. **Sederma primary documents.** I read the 2013 brochure through a distributor's upload. The older 24/25-volunteer data are known only through CIR and an
   archived third-party page. Asking our supplier for the current Croda/Sederma technical dossier would confirm the figures and may add newer studies (for
   example, men's panels).
8. **Trademark use.** Matrixyl® and Matrixyl® 3000 are Sederma (Croda) trademarks. Confirm with the supplier that our product names and the ® usage are permitted. Sederma runs a co-branding programme ("matrixylinside.com" is printed on the 2013 brochure).
9. **Gorouhi & Maibach 2009** (the only systematic review of controlled topical-peptide studies): full text not accessed. It may hold a critical appraisal worth quoting on the hub page.
10. **Newer trials, 2018–2026:** none found for Matrixyl 3000 alone. The only newer items are multi-ingredient (Yang 2024, Cayce 2026 10-peptide serum, open-label) and cannot be attributed to the peptides.

### 8.4 Wording rules from this check

- **+256%:** always "in lab tests on skin cells" and "against cells given only the solvent". Say "at the highest dose tested" wherever the figure stands alone (8.7, decision 2).
- **Face versus forearm.** The +15% density is a **forearm** result. The face result is the fibre network beside the eye corner: **+13.2% more than the
  placebo side at 2 months (p<0.05)**, by confocal microscopy. Write it as "a less fragmented fibre network just beneath the surface". Never call it
  "collagen", because the patent measures fibre circumference. Never write "repairs" or "rebuilds the dermis", although the patent says it: that wording is
  medicinal in our prose.
- **Say "just beneath the skin's surface", not "upper skin layer".** The measured band is the top of the dermis.
- **Skin samples:** direction only. Write "more collagen I, IV, VII and XVII than with placebo, in donated skin samples (manufacturer data, no figures published)", with no numbers.
- **Two different 39% figures.** Matrixyl 3000's −39.4% is deep-wrinkle **area** (2 months, 23 women). Tripeptide-1's −39% is wrinkle **length** (4 weeks, 15 women). Never merge them.
- **Robinson:** "small but significant from week 8" is its honest strength. No percentage exists, and the facial area measured is not stated. It is a claim for the **serum only**.
- **Katayama:** "fibroblasts, the cells that make collagen, grown in the lab". Write "a piece cut from procollagen as collagen is made; the authors proposed that such pieces tell cells to make more", not "released as collagen is broken down".
- **Percentage:** "the two peptides are well under 0.1% of the supplied solution; at the 3% the manufacturer recommends, a cream holds a few parts per million of peptide". CIR's "typical use below 10 ppm" is citable; the 100 + 50 ppm figure is not.
- **"Independent evidence"** takes only independent sources: CIR, and the L'Oréal lab study. Trzaska 2026 has No7/Boots co-authors, so call it "a university study" and keep it out of that line.
- **Cite CIR 2018 (the journal report), never the 2014 draft.**

**Findings cards:**

- **f1** _Deep Wrinkles: −39% in 2 Months (Manufacturer Half-Face Study)_. Bold first line: 3% Matrixyl 3000 cream on one half of the face and placebo cream
  on the other, twice a day for 2 months (23 women aged 42–67). Bullets: area taken up by deep wrinkles −39%; main-wrinkle depth −20%, volume −23%; roughness
  −16%. Close: the placebo half showed no significant change in wrinkle area, depth or roughness; each half was compared with its own start; these are the
  manufacturer's figures, not peer-reviewed. Image: before/after (8.5).
- **f2** _Fine Lines Reduced vs Placebo in 93 Women: Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4 (Double-Blind Trial)_. In a 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled half-face
  trial, a moisturiser with 3 ppm palmitoyl pentapeptide-4 (the original Matrixyl®) reduced wrinkles and fine lines significantly more than the same
  moisturiser without it, measured by image analysis and expert graders. The effect was small but significant from week 8. Procter & Gamble scientists ran the
  trial. It is in our Firming Serum, not the cream. Image: before/after (8.5).
- **f3** _Firmer-Looking Skin Beneath the Surface: +15% vs Placebo_. 28 women aged 51–72; 3% Matrixyl 3000 on half the face and one forearm, placebo on the
  other side, for 2 months. Ultrasound showed the layer just beneath the surface 15% denser than on the placebo arm (p<0.01), with a 7.8% difference already
  at 1 month. On the face, confocal imaging beside the eye showed a less fragmented fibre network, 13.2% ahead of the placebo side at 2 months (p<0.05).
  Sederma's scientists reported the same direction in a peer-reviewed journal (Mondon et al., 2015). Image: today's card-1 cross-section, and **never a face
  photo**.
- **f4** _Two Peptides Together: Collagen I +256% in Lab Tests_. The patent figures (+256%, fibronectin +147%, hyaluronic acid +92%; tetrapeptide-7 alone
  +65%, tripeptide-1 alone −9%), at the highest dose tested and against the solvent. In donated skin samples, Sederma reports more collagen I, IV, VII and
  XVII than with placebo (no figures published). An independent L'Oréal lab found each peptide raised type III collagen in UV-exposed skin cells (Zang et al.,
  2026). Close: results in cells and skin samples, not in living skin.
- **f5** _Assessed Safe in Cosmetics by an Independent Panel_. CIR 2018: both peptides are safe as used, typically below 10 ppm. CIR 2024: palmitoyl
  pentapeptide-4 is safe as used, and a patch test in 51 people found no irritation or sensitisation. It was "well tolerated" in the 93-woman trial. Close: a
  finding about the ingredients, not a test of our products; patch-test first.
