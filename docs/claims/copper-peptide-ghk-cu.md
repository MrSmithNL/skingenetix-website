# Claims register: Copper peptide GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide-1)

> **Status 2026-09-23:** the hub was brought level with the Matrixyl and glutathione builds (`configs/hub-upgrades/copper-peptide-research-parity-2026-09-23.json`): key figures at the top (−24.1% vs −15.0%; 7 in 10; the 2026 review), at-a-glance list, ten-row evidence table naming the funder and the journal's weaker peer review beside Badenhorst, image rows, two FAQs. Verification log at the end. Gaps 1–3 (no stated concentration, no tolerability data at 2%, the unread Leyden abstracts) are unchanged.

**Built:** 2026-09-22 · **Scope:** Skingenetix Copper Peptide (GHK-Cu) 2% Renewal Serum, 2% Day Gel-Cream, 2% Night Cream, the microneedling stamp set, and bundles.
**Standing rule:** use the strongest claim the best evidence supports. It has to be true, sourced, and within EU Regulation 655/2013 (the common criteria: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making).
**Verification standard:** I checked every number below against the primary text (full text or abstract) on 2026-09-22, unless the row says otherwise. Where I could not open the primary source, the row says so and the grade is lowered.

**Grades:** **A** = controlled human study with instrument measurement · **B** = uncontrolled or self-assessed human data, or a conference abstract of a controlled study · **C** = ex vivo (excised human skin) · **D** = in vitro or animal.

> **Condition that applies to every claim here:** none of the human studies states the GHK-Cu concentration it used (Badenhorst 2016 does not give one, and the Leyden, Abdulghani and Miller abstracts give none). **No claim can say "clinically proven at 2%" or suggest that our product was the one tested.** Every claim is an **ingredient claim**: "GHK-Cu, the peptide in this serum, …", "In a double-blind trial, GHK-Cu …". Under 655/2013 (criterion 3, evidential support), ingredient evidence has to be relevant to the finished product. The honest way to do this is to name the ingredient as the subject of the study and keep the product out of the results sentence.

## 1. Headline claims, ready to use (ranked)

### Claim 1 (hero). GHK-Cu beat the vehicle it was tested in, head to head
**Claim:** *"In a double-blind trial, copper peptide GHK-Cu reduced wrinkle volume 55.8% more than the same serum without it."*
Short form: *"Double-blind tested: 55.8% more wrinkle-volume reduction than the serum without GHK-Cu."*

- **Fact:** Randomised, double-blind, split-face trial. 40 women aged 40–65 (39 analysed), 8 weeks, twice daily. The GHK-Cu serum (GHK-Cu in a lipid nano-carrier) went on one side of the face, and the vehicle serum (no GHK-Cu, no nano-carrier) went on the other side in group 2 (n=20). Measurement was PRIMOS 3D fringe projection on one crow's-feet wrinkle per subject. **Wrinkle volume: 55.8% greater improvement vs vehicle, p<0.0001. Wrinkle depth: 32.8% greater improvement vs vehicle, p=0.0123** (Table 3).
- **What 55.8% is:** it is *relative* to the vehicle. It is **not** "wrinkle volume down 55.8%". Change from baseline at 8 weeks (Table 2): GHK-Cu serum volume −24.1% ±8.6 vs vehicle −15.0% ±5.2 (group 2). Depth −20.3% vs −15.3%.
- **Source:** Badenhorst T, Svirskis D, Merrilees M, Bolke L, Wu Z. *J Aging Sci* 2016;4(3):166. DOI 10.4172/2329-8847.1000166. Full text: https://www.walshmedicalmedia.com/open-access/effects-of-ghkcu-on-mmp-and-timp-expression-collagen-and-elastin-production-and-facial-wrinkle-parameters-2329-8847-1000166.pdf
- **Grade: A−.** It is controlled and instrumental. Four things lower it: it is a single study; it was funded by Snowberry NZ (maker of the test serum, which the paper says had no role in analysis, and independent statisticians were used); the trial was run at Dermatest GmbH; and the journal is published by the OMICS group (Walsh Medical Media), which has weaker peer review. A 2018 review cites it (Pickart & Margolina, PMC6073405), and so does the 2026 systematic review (claim 3).
- **Attach to:** serum, day gel-cream, night cream, bundles, **as an ingredient claim only**. **Conditions:** (a) say "a GHK-Cu serum" or "GHK-Cu", never "this serum"; (b) the tested formula used a nano-carrier, so do not claim ours works through the same delivery system; (c) keep "vs the serum without it" in the claim, because the bare "55.8%" is misleading.

### Claim 2. It outperformed a Matrixyl 3000 product on wrinkle volume
**Claim:** *"Head to head in a double-blind trial, GHK-Cu reduced wrinkle volume 31.6% more than a Matrixyl 3000 product."*

- **Fact:** Same trial, group 1 (n=19): GHK-Cu serum on one side, a commercial Matrixyl 3000 product on the other. **Wrinkle volume: 31.6% greater improvement, p=0.0044.** Change from baseline at 8 weeks: −25.8% vs −20.0%. **Wrinkle depth: 23.4% greater improvement, p=0.0577, which is NOT significant.**
- **Source / grade:** as for claim 1. **A−.**
- **Attach to:** as for claim 1. **Conditions:** (a) the claim covers **volume only**; never say GHK-Cu beat Matrixyl on depth; (b) the comparator was a single product. The paper names it Strivectin SD Advanced Intensive Concentrate. **Do not name that brand on our pages.** "A Matrixyl 3000 product" is fair and accurate. (c) **Check first** whether our "multi-peptide complex" contains Matrixyl 3000 (palmitoyl tripeptide-1 + palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7). If it does, this line reads oddly next to our own INCI list, and it would be better placed on the hub page than on the product page.

### Claim 3. An independent 2026 systematic review confirms the wrinkle result
**Claim:** *"A 2026 systematic review in Aesthetic Surgery Journal found that GHK-Cu 'significantly reduced wrinkle volume and depth compared with controls.'"*

- **Fact:** Mokhtar et al. is a PRISMA systematic review (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, searched through March 2026) covering 20 studies: 18 preclinical and **2 RCTs**. The abstract says: "Clinically, GHK-Cu improved patient-reported satisfaction after laser resurfacing and significantly reduced wrinkle volume and depth compared with controls." It also says the findings "were constrained by methodological variability and a limited number of well-designed clinical trials."
- **Source:** Mokhtar J, …, Lellouch AG. *Aesthet Surg J* 2026 Aug 20:sjag169. PMID 42619529. DOI 10.1093/asj/sjag169. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42619529/
- **Grade:** this is a review, so it adds no new data. Its value is that it is **independent** (Mayo Clinic, Cedars-Sinai, MBRU authors) and **peer-reviewed in a leading journal**. **I read the abstract only.** The full text is paywalled, so I have not confirmed which 2 RCTs it included, although the wording matches Badenhorst 2016 and Miller 2006.
- **Attach to:** hub page and product Clinical Research block. **Condition:** state that it covered two trials, or pair it with claim 1. Quoting the positive sentence without its "limited number of well-designed trials" sentence is selective; see section 2 for a fair pairing.

### Claim 4. It beat vitamin C and tretinoin in a skin-biopsy study
**Claim:** *"In a skin-biopsy study, 7 in 10 people using a copper-peptide cream showed new collagen production after one month, compared with 5 in 10 on vitamin C and 4 in 10 on tretinoin."*

- **Fact, from the primary abstract:** 20 healthy volunteers, creams applied to the thighs (one cream per thigh) for **one month**, immunohistology of biopsies. "A significant increase of procollagen synthesis by dermal papillary fibroblasts from baseline in 4 of 10 volunteers treated with tretinoin, 5 of 10 treated with vitamin C, 5 of 10 treated with melatonin and 7 of 10 healthy volunteers treated with the copper-binding peptide cream."
- **Source:** Abdulghani AA, Sherr A, Shirin S, Solodkina G, Morales Tapia E, Wolf B, Gottlieb AB. *Disease Management and Clinical Outcomes* 1998;1(4):136–141. DOI 10.1016/s1088-3371(98)00011-4. Abstract: https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/effects-of-topical-creams-containing-vitamin-c-a-copper-binding-p-2/ (Mount Sinai). It was also reported as a meeting abstract in *J Invest Dermatol* 1998;110:686 (per Pickart's "Copperceuticals" paper).
- **Grade: B.** It is a human biopsy study, small (10 per treatment), with **no statistical test between treatments**. The comparison is a count of responders. The concentration is not given. The skin tested was thigh, not face.
- **Attach to:** serum, day, night (the tested product was a cream). **Conditions:** (a) say **one month** (Pickart 2018 says 12 weeks, which is wrong); (b) say "7 in 10 people", **not** "70% more collagen"; (c) do not say "outperforms" or "better than retinoids", because no significance test compared the groups. Setting the three counts side by side is accurate and still persuasive. (d) **EU flag:** "collagen production" describes a physiological action. As a report of a study ("in a study, … showed") it is defensible. As a product promise ("boosts your collagen") it is borderline medicinal. Keep it in study-report form.

### Claim 5. 12-week facial studies in 71 and 41 women
**Claim:** *"In 12-week studies presented at the American Academy of Dermatology, a copper-peptide face cream improved the look of firmness, clarity and fine lines in 71 women, and a copper-peptide eye cream outperformed both placebo and a vitamin K cream in 41 women."*

- **Fact, as reported by the reviews:** Face cream: 71 women with mild to advanced photoaging, 12 weeks. It "improved skin laxity, clarity, and appearance, reduced fine lines and the depth of wrinkles, and increased skin density and thickness" (Pickart 2015). Eye cream: 41 women, 12 weeks, "compared to a placebo control and an eye cream containing vitamin K. The GHK-Cu cream performed better than both controls in terms of reducing lines and wrinkles, improving overall appearance, and increasing skin density and thickness" (Pickart 2015; Pickart 2018 says the same).
- **Sources:** Leyden J, Stephens T, Finkey M, Appa Y, Barkovic S. AAD 60th Annual Meeting, Feb 2002, abstracts P68 (face) and P69 (eye). These were never published as full papers. Secondary reports: Pickart 2015, PMC4508379 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4508379/); Pickart 2018, PMC6073405; Mortazavi 2024, PMC11830136.
- **Grade: B,** and it is secondary. **I could not access the primary abstracts,** so no effect sizes, p-values or concentrations are available. Pickart calls these studies "placebo-controlled". The eye-cream control is confirmed by all three reviews; the face-cream control is confirmed only by Pickart. The reviews' authors are from Skin Biology, a GHK-Cu product company. The sponsor of the AAD work is not stated in anything I could read.
- **Attach to:** day gel-cream and night cream (the tested product was a cream). The eye-cream result can only be used for an eye product, and we do not sell one. **Conditions:** no numbers other than n and duration; say "presented at" rather than "published in"; and use "the look of", because the cosmetic register is required here.

### Claim 6. The body makes this peptide itself
**Claim:** *"GHK-Cu isn't borrowed from a lab. It's a peptide your own body makes, first isolated from human plasma in 1973."*
Optional add-on: *"Levels in the blood fall from about 200 ng/mL at age 20 to about 80 ng/mL by age 60."*

- **Fact:** "It naturally occurs in human plasma, saliva, and urine. In plasma the level of GHK is about 200 ng/mL (10⁻⁷ M) at age 20, but declines to 80 ng/mL by age 60." The 1973 isolation is Pickart's UCSF PhD thesis.
- **Source:** Pickart 2015 (PMC4508379) and 2018 (PMC6073405), full text.
- **Grade:** the origin fact is solid (thesis plus Nature 1980, PMID 7453802, "Growth-modulating plasma tripeptide may function by facilitating copper uptake into cells", as cited). **The 200 → 80 ng/mL figures have no citation in Pickart 2015,** and the authors sell GHK-Cu products. Rate the decline figure as review-level only.
- **Attach to:** all products and the hub. **Conditions:** (a) do **not** imply that applying GHK-Cu to the skin "restores" or "replenishes" what the body loses. Nothing tests that, and "restores" leans medicinal. (b) It is plasma, not skin, so do not write "your skin loses 60% of its GHK".

### Claim 7. Microneedling multiplies delivery (stamp set)
**Claim:** *"Pair it with the stamp: in lab tests on human skin, microneedle pre-treatment let 134 nanomoles of GHK-Cu through in 9 hours, while almost none passed intact skin."*

- **Fact, verbatim from the abstract:** "In 9 h, 134 ± 12 nanomoles of peptide and 705 ± 84 nanomoles of copper permeated though the microneedle treated human skin, while almost no peptide or copper permeated through intact human skin. No obvious signs of skin irritation were observed with the use of GHK-Cu after microneedle pretreatment" (irritation was tested in cellular and porcine models).
- **Source:** Li H, Low YS, Chong HP, Zin MT, Lee CY, Li B, Leolukman M, Kang L. *Pharm Res* **2015** Aug;32(8):2678–89 (the Hairgenetix register says 2016; PubMed says 2015 Aug, epub Feb 2015). PMID 25690343. DOI 10.1007/s11095-015-1652-z.
- **Grade: C** (excised human skin, polymeric microneedle array, not our stamp).
- **Attach to:** stamp set, and bundles that pair the stamp with a GHK-Cu product. **Conditions:** (a) this measures **how much** passed through, not **how deep** it went, so never write "20× deeper" or "20×" (the Hairgenetix lesson); (b) the needle geometry was not ours; (c) the safety line covers cells and pigs, not people.

### Claim 8 (conditional). Paired with hyaluronic acid for a reason (serum only)
**Claim:** *"GHK-Cu and hyaluronic acid work better together: in lab-tested human skin, the pair doubled collagen IV, a key protein of the layer where the epidermis meets the dermis."*

- **Fact:** GHK-Cu plus low-molecular-weight HA at a **1:9 ratio** raised collagen IV ×25.4 in fibroblasts and **×2.03 in ex vivo skin**.
- **Source:** Jiang F, Wu Y, Liu Z, Hong M, Huang Y. *J Cosmet Dermatol* 2023;22(9):2598–2604. PMID 37062921. The authors are from ingredient makers (Bloomage/Peptites).
- **Grade: C.**
- **Attach to:** **serum only**, and **only if** our HA includes a low-molecular-weight fraction. Otherwise say "a pairing studied in the lab" and drop the ×2.03. The 1:9 ratio will not match ours at 2% GHK-Cu, so never say "at the ratio shown to double collagen IV".

## 2. Replacements for the flat line

Current line: *"For GHK-Cu, most of the evidence comes from laboratory studies; published trials in people are small."*

**Option A (recommended, leads with the best trial):**
> "In a double-blind, split-face trial, 40 women used a GHK-Cu serum on one side of the face for 8 weeks. It reduced wrinkle volume 55.8% more than the same serum without GHK-Cu, and 31.6% more than a Matrixyl 3000 product, measured by 3D skin imaging. A 2026 systematic review found the same: GHK-Cu significantly reduced wrinkle volume and depth compared with controls. The human evidence is still only a handful of trials."

**Option B (breadth, "decades of science"):**
> "GHK-Cu has been studied for more than 50 years, since it was first isolated from human plasma in 1973. The lab evidence is strong enough that an independent 2024 review concluded 'GHK can be considered as an anti-wrinkle ingredient.' Human studies back it up: a double-blind wrinkle trial (40 women, 8 weeks), a skin-biopsy study where 7 in 10 people produced more collagen, and 12-week facial studies in 71 and 41 women."
> Quote source: Mortazavi 2024, PMC11830136: "Based on cellular studies, undoubtedly, GHK can be considered as an anti-wrinkle ingredient." These are independent authors (Shahid Beheshti University), with no conflicts declared. The quote is scoped to cellular studies, and "Human studies back it up" in the copy says that.

**Option C (compact, for a small block):**
> "Tested against Matrixyl 3000 in a double-blind trial, GHK-Cu reduced wrinkle volume 31.6% more, and 55.8% more than the serum without it (8 weeks, 40 women)."

**Honesty note:** Mortazavi 2024 still says "a surprising absence of clinical studies". Options A and B are true and not misleading because they name the actual trials and their sizes. Do not add "one of the most-studied peptides". If a trailing caveat is wanted, "Human trials so far are few but consistent" is accurate. Miller 2006 is the only null result, and it was a post-laser setting, not everyday use.

## 4. Claims to AVOID

| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| "Clinically proven at 2%" / "2% is the clinically studied dose" / "0.5–2% studied range" | No human study states its concentration. The range has no source (removed from the hub 2026-09-22). |
| "Reduces wrinkles by 55.8%" / "wrinkle volume −56%" | 55.8% is **relative to the vehicle**. The absolute change was −24% to −26% at 8 weeks. |
| "Reduces wrinkle depth more than Matrixyl" | p=0.0577, not significant. |
| "Our serum beat Matrixyl" / naming Strivectin | The trial tested another company's formulation. Naming a competitor brand is a comparative-advertising risk. |
| "70% more collagen" / "collagen +70%" | Abdulghani = **7 of 10 people**, not a 70% increase. Pickart 2018's "collagen synthesis, 70%" is **cell culture with LED light**. The Hairgenetix stats register line "collagen synthesis up to +70%" should be re-checked. |
| "Outperforms retinol / tretinoin" | No between-group statistics; 10 people per arm; thigh skin; one month. |
| "12-week collagen study" (Abdulghani) | It was one month (primary abstract). |
| "One of the most-studied peptides" | Contradicted by Mortazavi 2024 (removed 2026-09-22). |
| "Clinically proven" (unqualified) | Only one controlled instrumental trial isolates GHK-Cu. "Double-blind tested" and "clinically tested ingredient" are accurate; "proven" overstates. |
| "Stimulates / boosts collagen synthesis in your skin", "regenerates", "heals", "repairs", "restores", "wound healing", "anti-inflammatory" | These can read as medicinal under the Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 (borderline with the medicines directive). Also, by Pickart's own account, GHK-Cu **failed FDA wound-healing trials**. |
| "Resets 4,000 genes" / "affects 31.2% of human genes" / "resets your DNA" | Connectivity Map cell-line data; medicinal and misleading on a product page. |
| "Calms redness", "soothes", "repairs the barrier", "speeds recovery after lasers or microneedling" | Miller 2006 found **no difference in redness** after laser. The Maibach barrier and irritation studies used **soy-derived mixed copper peptides**, and their abstracts do not name the agent. |

| "20× deeper" / "20× more absorption" (microneedling) | Li 2015 measured amount, not depth, and intact skin was "almost none", so no ratio can be calculated (Hairgenetix HG-114 lesson). |

| "Effective in trials in people for scars, dandruff, melasma" | Cureus and Korean studies are combinations or uncontrolled; GHK-Cu's contribution cannot be separated out. |

## 5. Safety and tolerability evidence, exactly as reported

- **Badenhorst 2016 (the only controlled facial trial):** "well tolerated by 39 of the 40 subjects throughout the eight-week application period. One participant experienced minor unwanted skin reactions on both the right and left side of their face after application of the NRFS serum and SSID. This subject ceased application and symptoms resolved without medical treatment." The authors add that none of the tretinoin-type side effects (peeling, dryness, erythema) occurred. The concentration is unknown.
- **Leyden 2002 face study:** Badenhorst describes it as showing improvements "without irritation". That is secondary; the primary is not accessible.
- **Li 2015:** "No obvious signs of skin irritation were observed with the use of GHK-Cu after microneedle pretreatment." That was in **cellular and porcine models**, not people.
- **Patel 2025 (Cureus, combination scar gel):** "without adverse effects". This is open-label and uses a multi-ingredient product.
- **CIR Expert Panel (2014 / IJT 2018):** copper tripeptide-1 is "safe in the present practices of use and concentration in cosmetics". **But** its use concentration was **not reported** (18 uses), the highest concentration in the whole peptide survey was **0.002%**, and "typical use concentrations … are < 10 ppm". **The report contains no human irritation or sensitisation test on copper tripeptide-1 itself.** All the human patch data are for palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl hexapeptide-12 trade materials. **Our 2% is 20,000 ppm, about 1,000× the highest concentration the Panel saw for any of these peptides, so the CIR conclusion does not cover it.** The report also notes that commercial GHK-Cu is "approximately 95% pure, but often includes small amounts of mildly neurotoxic materials" (tested by intracranial injection in mice). That has no bearing on topical safety, but it is a reason to keep supplier CoA purity on file.

**Verdict:**
- **"Well tolerated in clinical testing"**: supportable **for the ingredient in the Badenhorst trial**, worded as "In an 8-week trial, a GHK-Cu serum was well tolerated by 39 of 40 women." It must not be stated about our 2% products.
- **"Gentle" / "suitable for sensitive skin" / "non-irritating" / "hypoallergenic"**: **no evidence.** Only a product-level HRIPT (repeat-insult patch test) or an in-use test of **our** formulas can support these. That test is the single highest-value piece of evidence we could buy (see Gaps).
- The EU product information file (PIF) and safety assessment (CPSR) for the 2% products must cover 2% with its own data. If it relies on the CIR, that is a gap.

## 6. Gaps

1. **No human study at a stated concentration**, so nothing links 2% to an outcome. A product-level in-use study of our serum (8–12 weeks, instrument-measured, e.g. Antera/PRIMOS plus cutometer, 20–30 people) would give first-party numbers that attach directly to the products.
2. **No tolerability data at 2%.** A HRIPT (about 50–100 subjects) plus a dermatologist-supervised in-use test would unlock "dermatologically tested" and possibly "suitable for sensitive skin". It should also confirm what the CPSR relies on.
3. **I could not read the primary Leyden/Appa AAD 2002 abstracts, the Finkley 2005 chapter, or the full Mokhtar 2026 review.** A library copy of the Finkley chapter (Marcel Dekker 2005, pp. 549–563) may contain effect sizes for the 71-, 41- and 67-woman studies. Those would be the strongest numbers we could add.
4. **No human data for GHK-Cu + vitamin C, GHK-Cu + niacinamide, or GHK-Cu + microneedling on the face.** The only microneedling evidence is ex vivo (Li 2015). Scalp work (Kuceki 2025) is a combination and belongs to Hairgenetix.
5. **Copper + ascorbic-acid compatibility in our serum:** the literature says nothing about whether 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid and GHK-Cu remain stable together. That is a formulation question, not a claim, but avoid any "synergy" claim.
6. **Journal quality of the key trial:** the best controlled result appeared in an OMICS-group journal. If a competitor or regulator challenges it, the Mokhtar 2026 systematic review is the independent peer-reviewed support.
7. **Check against our INCI list:** if the "multi-peptide complex" contains Matrixyl 3000, move claim 2 to the hub rather than the product page.
8. **Hairgenetix follow-up:** its verified-stats register shows "collagen synthesis up to +70%" against Pickart 2015/2018. That is the LED + GHK cell-culture figure and should be corrected there. Its claims register dates Li as 2016; PubMed says 2015.
