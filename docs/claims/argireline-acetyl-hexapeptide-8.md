# Claims register — Argireline® (acetyl hexapeptide-8 / acetyl hexapeptide-3)

**Product:** Skingenetix Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 Anti-Wrinkle Serum, **10% Argireline®** (Lubrizol/Lipotec trade name, confirmed by Malcolm 2026-09-22). Also in two bundles with Matrixyl 3000.
**Compiled:** 2026-09-22. Every figure below was read at source (abstract and/or full text) on that date, unless it is marked otherwise.
**Legal frame:** Regulation (EU) 655/2013 common criteria (legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making). The product is a cosmetic. Claims must describe the **appearance** of skin. They must not claim to change a body function (muscles, nerves) and must not compare the product with a medicine or an injection.

Grades: **A** = controlled human study with instrumental measures · **B** = uncontrolled or self-assessed human data, or manufacturer in-vivo data · **C** = ex vivo (excised skin) · **D** = in vitro or animal.

## ⚠️ Read first: two facts that change how every number is used

1. **"10% Argireline®" means 10% of the peptide *solution*, not 10% peptide.** Tadini 2015 states that Argireline is a "solution containing 0.05% acetyl hexapeptide-3", used "to a concentration of 10%". The finished product therefore holds about **0.005% peptide**. Lungu 2013 (NIH) used 0.005% and called it the level "previously used in cosmetic applications". Wang 2013, Raikou 2017 and Tadini 2015 all used **10% Argireline**, so **our serum matches the clinical dose**. That is our strongest single fact.
   - Lubrizol lists the Argireline® peptide solution NP INCI as *Water (Aqua), Acetyl Hexapeptide-8, Phenoxyethanol, Potassium Sorbate*, with a **recommended use level of 2–10%**. We are at the top of that range.
   - **Action:** get the supplier's TDS or certificate of analysis to confirm (a) the grade (NP, C, or *Amplified*, which is a different product with a 2–5% range) and (b) the actual peptide content. Until that is done, do not print "0.005%" as a fact about our serum.
   - **Live-copy flag:** the hub page (`configs/hub-upgrades/acetyl-hexapeptide-8-research.json`) says Blanes-Mira used "an emulsion with **10% of the peptide**". The abstract does say "10% of the hexapeptide", but that paper names the hexapeptide itself "Argireline", and every later study uses 10% of the *solution*. Rewrite it as **"an emulsion with 10% Argireline"**, which is correct under either reading. The FAQ line "contains 10% Argireline® (acetyl hexapeptide-8)" is accurate, but readers may take it as 10% peptide. Consider "10% Argireline® peptide solution".
2. **The 48.9% is a count of people, not a percentage of wrinkle.** Wang 2013 full text: "The total anti-wrinkle efficacy was calculated as the percentage of subjects who were graded 3 or 4 on the global assessment of improvement scale [0 = no change … 4 = 100% improved] after 4 weeks." In other words, **22 of 45 people** (48.9%) were graded 3 or 4, against 0 of 15 on placebo. At least two published reviews misread this figure: **Zdrada-Nowak 2025** ("a 49% reduction in wrinkle depth") and **Aung 2026, J Cosmet Dermatol** ("48.9% reduction in periorbital wrinkle depth"). Never cite either review for this number. Cite Wang directly.

**Supporting line, not a headline:** Tadini 2015 found that 10% Argireline (0.05% peptide solution) reduced facial skin **anisotropy** after 4 weeks against the vehicle (p < 0.05; 40 women aged 35–55; Reviscometer). In plain terms, skin became more uniform in how it resists stretching in different directions, which is a feature of younger skin. Elasticity (Cutometer) did **not** change. Usable as "helps skin look more supple and even" only with care, because it is a technical measure.

**How to phrase the mechanism (cosmetic-safe):** "Argireline® is a peptide modelled on part of SNAP-25, a protein involved in expression. It is designed to **soften the look of expression lines** such as crow's feet and forehead lines." Keep the SNARE and neuron detail on the learning hub, framed as *laboratory* findings, as the current hub copy already does.

**Bundles with Matrixyl 3000:** **no study of Argireline + Matrixyl 3000 exists** that I could find. Raikou 2017 is often quoted as the "combination" study, but it paired Argireline with **tripeptide-10 citrulline (Decorinyl®)**, not Matrixyl. Even there, the combination was not better than Argireline alone at day 20. Allowed wording: "two complementary peptides — Argireline® for the look of expression lines, Matrixyl 3000 for the look of firmness." **Not allowed:** "synergistic", "clinically proven together", or any combined percentage.

| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| "Botox in a bottle", "Botox-like", "like Botox without needles", "alternative to Botox/injections" | This compares a cosmetic with a prescription medicine and implies a medicinal effect (655/2013 honesty and fairness criteria; Directive 2001/83 borderline). BOTOX® is also a registered trademark. The only independent objective test (Henseler 2023) concluded Argireline "is not deemed to be an alternative treatment to botulinum toxin". |
| "Relaxes facial muscles", "blocks nerve signals", "stops muscle contractions", "paralyses wrinkles", "neuromodulator" | This claims to change a physiological function, which makes the product medicinal. No study shows it happens when the peptide is applied to the skin (FDA/Kraeling found none in the dermis; Zdrada-Nowak says it "remains uncertain"). |

| "30% fewer wrinkles in 30 days" (no "up to", no attribution) | The source says "up to 30%" and is the developers' own small study. |
| "Clinically proven serum", "our serum reduced wrinkles by…" | Our finished product has not been tested. All the data is on the ingredient, at a matching concentration. |

## 4. Safety and tolerability (as reported)

- **Topical, controlled studies:** no adverse effects in Wang 2013 (45 active users, 4 weeks around the eyes), Raikou 2017 (no warmth, stinging, redness, peeling, itching or ocular irritation), Tadini 2015 (none reported) or Henseler 2023 ("no significant adverse events, allergic reactions, or skin irritations").
- **Eyelid use (Lungu 2013, 0.005%):** 4 cases of mild, self-limiting eyelid heaviness or blepharitis, **2 on active and 2 on placebo**. Investigators put this down to the thick cream, not the peptide. The paper cites manufacturer toxicology showing "no topical or systemic toxicity at concentrations of 0.05% and above" (BCN Peptides data on file, not public).
- **Developers:** "did not exhibit in vivo oral toxicity nor primary irritation at high doses" (Blanes-Mira 2002). Wang 2013 cites acute toxicity at ≥2000 mg/kg and a negative Ames test from the same source.
- **Penetration as a safety point:** stays in the stratum corneum and epidermis; none detected in the dermis (Kraeling 2015, US FDA).
- **In vitro:** cytotoxicity only at very high concentrations (Grosicki 2014).
- **Injection is a different matter:** a case of *Mycobacterium abscessus* infection after Argireline was **injected** into the forehead and temples (Chen 2021, [PubMed 33748252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33748252/)). Never suggest injecting, or using the serum with DIY microneedling.
- **Stability:** the methionine in Argireline oxidises in finished cosmetics (Kluczyk 2021, [PubMed 33482052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33482052/)). This is a formulation QA point, not a claim.

## 5. Gaps

1. **Our finished serum has not been tested.** Every claim is ingredient-level and relies on matching concentration (10% Argireline) and a similar vehicle (most studies used O/W emulsions; ours is a serum). A small consumer-perception or instrumental test of our own product would unlock "our serum" claims.
2. **Supplier documents are missing.** The Lubrizol technical dossier and in-vivo reports are behind a request form. We still need: the grade (NP, C or Amplified), the peptide content, and the full 7-day FOITS report, including whether 20.6% is against baseline or placebo.
3. **Blanes-Mira 2002 full text was not read** (Wiley paywall). The n, the vehicle result and the exact concentration are unverified. The "17% at 15 days / 27% at 30 days" figures circulate online, and Wang 2013 quotes them inconsistently (17% and 30%, plus a separate "5% cream, 27%" attributed to Lupo 2007). Do not use any of them until the full text is obtained.
4. **Wang 2013's instrumental data cannot be quantified.** Table 2 has no baseline and no between-group test, so the only usable numbers are the responder rate and the p-values.
5. **The independent evidence is thin and mixed.** Positive: Wang (the distributor supplied the product), Raikou (n ≈ 6/arm), Tadini (indirect measure). Null: Henseler (the only independent Visia study; concentration not stated). No trial has more than 60 people, and none lasts longer than 60 days.
6. **No Argireline + Matrixyl 3000 study exists.** The bundle copy must stay mechanism-based.
7. **No evidence that the "Botox-like" mechanism happens on skin.** The mechanism is shown in vitro only. The anti-wrinkle effect is probably epidermal (Zdrada-Nowak 2025).
8. **Demographics:** mostly women aged 25–60. Wang included 10 men (7 active), with no subgroup analysis. There is no data for darker skin tones specifically.
9. **Hwang et al.** ("100 ppm AH-8 inhibited muscle contractions by 26%" in a cell and *C. elegans* model) is quoted in Zdrada-Nowak 2025. It was **not verified at source**, and it would be a medicinal-sounding claim anyway. Do not use.
