# Claims register: topical glutathione (GSSG / GSH)

> **Status 2026-09-23: applied.** The hub was rebuilt to the research-page standard and every §2 verdict below was applied on the hub, the serum, the Duo, their FAQ items and `/pages/brightening-glow`, in six languages. Every figure was re-read at source that day before publishing (§7). The one open item is the parked formula fact (§6.1): the trial's magnitudes stay on the hub until the certificate confirms 2% GSSG in the serum.

**Product:** Glutathione 2% Brightening Serum (30 ml, €44). The live copy says "2% Glutathione (GSSG)", i.e. oxidised glutathione. The serum also contains 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, niacinamide, nonapeptide-1, tetrapeptide-1 and hyaluronic acid. It is also sold in the Brightening & Glow Duo with the Copper Peptide Day Gel-Cream.
**Pages audited, live, 2026-09-22:** `/products/glutathione-brightening-serum`, `/pages/glutathione-research`, `/pages/brightening-glow`, `/products/brightening-glow-duo-set-glutathione-serum-copper-peptide-ghk-cu-day-gel-cream`
**Method:** every number below was read in the source itself. For Watanabe 2014 (PMC4207440), Khanna 2025 (PMC12710870), Sonthalia 2018 (PMC5808366), Weschawalit 2017 (PMC5413479) and three 2025–26 reviews that means the full text. For the other sources it means the PubMed or publisher abstract, and those rows are marked **(abstract only)**. PubMed E-utilities and Europe PMC were used, with web search as a fallback.
**Legal frame:** Regulation (EU) 655/2013, the six common criteria: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making. A cosmetic claim describes how skin **looks**. Wording that says a product changes how the skin works ("inhibits melanin production"), or that it treats a condition (melasma, hyperpigmentation disorder), pushes it towards being a medicine.

**Evidence grades:** **A** = controlled human study with instrument measurements, topical. **B** = human topical study that is uncontrolled, self-assessed, or reported only in a conference abstract. **C** = ex vivo (skin samples outside the body). **D** = in vitro (cells in a dish). **X** = oral or IV only, which **cannot** support a claim about a product you put on your skin.

## ⚠️ Read this first: four facts that change the copy

1. **Only one controlled trial has tested topical GSSG on its own on skin tone: Watanabe et al. 2014.** Every review traces back to it. It was run by **Kyowa Hakko Bio**, the company that makes GSSG. Three of the four authors worked for Kyowa, but the paper says "no conflicts of interest". Say so wherever we cite it, the same way the copper page now names Skin Biology.
2. **Watanabe did not measure evenness, and its volunteers did not have uneven skin.** Table 2 of the paper shows that **100% of the 30 women had "homogeneous" facial skin colour at baseline**, and the main outcome was the *melanin index* (how much pigment is in the skin, read by a Mexameter probe) on the cheekbone. So the study shows **brighter / lighter-looking skin**. It does **not** show "more even tone" or "fades the look of dark spots". The live line "more even-looking skin tone vs placebo (p < 0.001)" swaps in an outcome that was never tested.
3. **The study did not measure "radiance", and blinded raters saw no difference in smoothness.** The skin-surface ("keratin") index measured smoother on the GSSG side from week 6 to week 10. But **neither the investigator nor the women themselves** scored the GSSG side as smoother. "Visibly smoother, more radiant-looking" is therefore wrong on both counts. "Measurably smoother texture" is right.
4. **The hub has rewritten the titles of four papers it cites.** For example, "Skin-whitening and skin-condition-improving effects…" appears as "Skin-condition-improving and even-tone effects…". A citation title is a bibliographic fact. Changing it misrepresents the source, and it also breaks the title match that AI crawlers use to connect our page to the paper. Either quote each title exactly as published, or drop titles and cite author, journal, year and PMID only. The citation of "skin-whitening" is not *our* claim, so the correct title can be shown.

## 4. Claims to AVOID

| Claim | Reason |
|---|---|
| "Skin whitening", "whitens", "lightens your skin colour" | The study's own word, but brand and ethical risk, and reviewers flag colourism (Khanna 2025). Use "brighter-looking". |
| "Inhibits melanin production / tyrosinase in your skin" | A claim about changing how the skin works (medicinal by function). The mechanism is also unproven for GSSG in vivo: Watanabe calls it "no more than speculation". |
| "Treats / fades melasma, hyperpigmentation, post-inflammatory marks" | A disease claim. The melasma data is microneedling-assisted (Mohamed 2023). |
| "Evens skin tone / fades dark spots, **clinically proven**" | Not measured for GSSG. Only Etnawati (GSH, abstract) touches dark spots. |
| "Visibly smoother" / "radiance clinically shown" / "firmer" / "more elastic" | Subjective smoothness: no difference. Radiance: not measured. Elasticity: no difference. |
| "Visible glow in days / 1–2 weeks" | No source. The first *perceived* change was week 4. |
| "Superior to hydroquinone" | Watanabe's discussion makes a cross-study comparison with a different population and duration. It is not evidence, and it would be comparative advertising. |
| "Clinically tested serum" / "our formula reduced pigment by 10.7%" | The trial tested a lotion, not our serum. |
| Any oral or IV glutathione result (Arjinpathana, Weschawalit, Handog, Wahab's oral arm) | Grade X. It cannot support a topical product. |
| Grandi's UV result framed as GSSG, or as "sun protection" | A different molecule. "Protects from UV" would also imply an SPF-type claim. |
| "Suitable for all skin types / sensitive skin / all skin tones", "non-irritating", "long-term safe" | One study, n = 30, types III–IV, 10 weeks. |
| "Most-studied" or "extensively researched" topical antioxidant | Khanna: "scientific research on topical glutathione is limited". |
| "Glutathione deeply penetrates / is absorbed into skin" | No published human penetration data for GSSG. |
| Watanabe's result described as "more even tone" | See fact #2 at the top of this register. |

## 5. Safety and tolerability, as reported

- **Watanabe 2014 (GSSG 2%, n = 30, 10 weeks):** all completed. One subject had mild whole-face redness on days 2–3 that resolved by day 4 without stopping treatment. "No adverse reactions related to GSSG". The authors add that "more studies will need to be accumulated… to determine the safety of its long-term use."
- **Etnawati 2019 (GSH 0.1/0.5%, n = 74):** mild tingling for a few minutes after the night cream. Acne worsened in **all** groups, including the group without GSH, so it points to the vehicle.
- **Dall'Oglio 2021 (GSH-C4 combination):** 3 of 30 stopped for poor skin tolerability. Not attributable to glutathione.
- **Khanna 2025:** "Safety data is limited… tolerability of creams and long-term effects sparsely analyzed."
- **Grandi 2019:** no grade 3–4 adverse events (n = 10).
- **IV glutathione** (context only; not our product): reviews report hepatotoxicity, renal and hypersensitivity concerns (Khanna 2025, Aloraini 2026). Sarkar 2025 calls IV use "contraindicated". Never let our copy blur topical into IV.
- **Safe product-page line:** "Well tolerated in its clinical trial of twice-daily use for 10 weeks. As with any new product, patch test first."

## 6. Gaps

1. **Is it really GSSG at 2%?** No INCI list is public and none is in the repo. Get the supplier CoA and INCI (and ideally confirm whether the raw material is Kyowa's AquaGluta®) before claim 1 is used.
2. **GSSG stability in our serum.** The trial used a simple lotion. Nothing shows how GSSG behaves alongside 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, niacinamide and peptides, or what strength remains at end of shelf life.
3. **Single study, run by the manufacturer, never replicated.** Watanabe is the only topical GSSG trial after 12 years. Its population was narrow (Filipino women, types III–IV, even skin, no sunscreen). There is no data on I–II or V–VI skin, on men, or on uneven or spotty skin.
4. **No evidence on evenness or dark spots for GSSG.** An own-product consumer or instrumental study (e.g. VISIA spots, n ≥ 30, 8–10 weeks) is the only way to earn "evens tone / dark spots" honestly.
5. **No follow-up after stopping.** Sonthalia and Sarkar call the effect "temporary" or "unsustainable". Durability is unknown.
6. **Penetration is unproven.** The only evidence is unpublished ("data not shown").
7. **Full texts not read:** Etnawati 2019 (PDF not retrieved; exact L* and lesion numbers unknown), Wahab 2021 (paywalled; topical strength and topical-alone result unverified), Sarkar 2025 (paywalled; 403). Grandi and Jung were read as abstracts only.
8. **"Most abundant intracellular antioxidant"** was not verified at source in this pass.
9. **Hub before/after image pair on the study card.** Whether the caption implies study photos is Malcolm's call (decision 2026-09-22, no AI disclosure). The label wording is the only thing flagged.
