3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, niacinamide, nonapeptide-1, tetrapeptide-1 and hyaluronic acid. It is also sold in the Brightening & Glow Duo with the Copper
Peptide Day Gel-Cream.
**Pages audited, live, 2026-09-22:** `/products/glutathione-brightening-serum`, `/pages/glutathione-research`, `/pages/brightening-glow`, `/products/brightening-glow-duo-set-glutathione-serum-copper-peptide-ghk-cu-day-gel-cream`
**Method:** every number below was read in the source itself. For Watanabe 2014 (PMC4207440), Khanna 2025 (PMC12710870), Sonthalia 2018 (PMC5808366),
Weschawalit 2017 (PMC5413479) and three 2025–26 reviews that means the full text. For the other sources it means the PubMed or publisher abstract, and
those rows are marked **(abstract only)**. PubMed E-utilities and Europe PMC were used, with web search as a fallback.
**Legal frame:** Regulation (EU) 655/2013, the six common criteria: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed
decision-making. A cosmetic claim describes how skin **looks**. Wording that says a product changes how the skin works ("inhibits melanin production"), or
that it treats a condition (melasma, hyperpigmentation disorder), pushes it towards being a medicine.

**Evidence grades:** **A** = controlled human study with instrument measurements, topical. **B** = human topical study that is uncontrolled, self-assessed,
or reported only in a conference abstract. **C** = ex vivo (skin samples outside the body). **D** = in vitro (cells in a dish). **X** = oral or IV only,
which **cannot** support a claim about a product you put on your skin.

## ⚠️ Read this first: four facts that change the copy

1. **Only one controlled trial has tested topical GSSG on its own on skin tone: Watanabe et al. 2014.** Every review traces back to it. It was run by
   **Kyowa Hakko Bio**, the company that makes GSSG. Three of the four authors worked for Kyowa, but the paper says "no conflicts of interest". Say so
   wherever we cite it, the same way the copper page now names Skin Biology.
2. **Watanabe did not measure evenness, and its volunteers did not have uneven skin.** Table 2 of the paper shows that **100% of the 30 women had
   "homogeneous" facial skin colour at baseline**, and the main outcome was the _melanin index_ (how much pigment is in the skin, read by a Mexameter
   probe) on the cheekbone. So the study shows **brighter / lighter-looking skin**. It does **not** show "more even tone" or "fades the look of dark
   spots". The live line "more even-looking skin tone vs placebo (p < 0.001)" swaps in an outcome that was never tested.
3. **The study did not measure "radiance", and blinded raters saw no difference in smoothness.** The skin-surface ("keratin") index measured smoother on
   the GSSG side from week 6 to week 10. But **neither the investigator nor the women themselves** scored the GSSG side as smoother. "Visibly smoother,
   more radiant-looking" is therefore wrong on both counts. "Measurably smoother texture" is right.
4. **The hub has rewritten the titles of four papers it cites.** For example, "Skin-whitening and skin-condition-improving effects…" appears as
   "Skin-condition-improving and even-tone effects…". A citation title is a bibliographic fact. Changing it misrepresents the source, and it also breaks
   the title match that AI crawlers use to connect our page to the paper. Either quote each title exactly as published, or drop titles and cite author,
   journal, year and PMID only. The citation of "skin-whitening" is not _our_ claim, so the correct title can be shown.

## 5. Safety and tolerability, as reported

- **Watanabe 2014 (GSSG 2%, n = 30, 10 weeks):** all completed. One subject had mild whole-face redness on days 2–3 that resolved by day 4 without stopping
  treatment. "No adverse reactions related to GSSG". The authors add that "more studies will need to be accumulated… to determine the safety of its
  long-term use."
- **Etnawati 2019 (GSH 0.1/0.5%, n = 74):** mild tingling for a few minutes after the night cream. Acne worsened in **all** groups, including the group without GSH, so it points to the vehicle.
- **Dall'Oglio 2021 (GSH-C4 combination):** 3 of 30 stopped for poor skin tolerability. Not attributable to glutathione.
- **Khanna 2025:** "Safety data is limited… tolerability of creams and long-term effects sparsely analyzed."
- **Grandi 2019:** no grade 3–4 adverse events (n = 10).
- **IV glutathione** (context only; not our product): reviews report hepatotoxicity, renal and hypersensitivity concerns (Khanna 2025, Aloraini 2026). Sarkar 2025 calls IV use "contraindicated". Never let our copy blur topical into IV.
- **Safe product-page line:** "Well tolerated in its clinical trial of twice-daily use for 10 weeks. As with any new product, patch test first."

## 6. Gaps

1. **Is it really GSSG at 2%?** No INCI list is public and none is in the repo. Get the supplier CoA and INCI (and ideally confirm whether the raw material is Kyowa's AquaGluta®) before claim 1 is used.
2. **GSSG stability in our serum.** The trial used a simple lotion. Nothing shows how GSSG behaves alongside 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, niacinamide and peptides, or what strength remains at end of shelf life.
3. **Single study, run by the manufacturer, never replicated.** Watanabe is the only topical GSSG trial after 12 years. Its population was narrow (Filipino women, types III–IV, even skin, no sunscreen). There is no data on I–II or V–VI skin, on men, or on uneven or spotty skin.
4. **No evidence on evenness or dark spots for GSSG.** An own-product consumer or instrumental study (e.g. VISIA spots, n ≥ 30, 8–10 weeks) is the only way to earn "evens tone / dark spots" honestly.
5. **No follow-up after stopping.** Sonthalia and Sarkar call the effect "temporary" or "unsustainable". Durability is unknown.
6. **Penetration is unproven.** The only evidence is unpublished ("data not shown").
7. **Full texts not read:** Etnawati 2019 (PDF not retrieved; exact L\* and lesion numbers unknown), Wahab 2021 (paywalled; topical strength and topical-alone result unverified), Sarkar 2025 (paywalled; 403). Grandi and Jung were read as abstracts only.
   **2026-09-26:** Etnawati and Wahab are now read in full (§8.1). Sarkar is still closed.
8. **"Most abundant intracellular antioxidant"** was not verified at source in this pass. **2026-09-26:** sourced as "the most abundant non-protein
   thiol that defends against oxidative stress" (Lu 2013, §8.1).
9. **Hub before/after image pair on the study card.** Whether the caption implies study photos is Malcolm's call (decision 2026-09-22, no AI disclosure). The label wording is the only thing flagged.

### 8.4 Wording rules from this check

- **Brightening only:** "brighter-looking skin", "lower skin pigment (melanin index)". Never "even tone", "dark spots", "radiance" or "whitening" in our
  prose (template §4 rule 6). The papers' "skin-whitening" stays only inside verbatim titles.
- **The ratings.** A score of 2 is the authors' threshold for a **visible** benefit: use it for "saw brighter-looking skin" (77% vs 23%; investigator
  67% vs 3%) and "visibly softer crow's feet" (a third vs none). A score of 1 or more is "at least a slight change": use it for timing ("87% by week 6,
  against 10%") and say "slight". Never attach a p-value to a rating.
- **Crow's feet:** the visible result rests on the blinded ratings. The instrument shows the glutathione side **lower than the placebo side** at weeks 6
  and 10, not a fall from the start, so never write "wrinkle depth −X%" or chart the curvature index as a reduction. The authors' own line,
  "effectively reduced wrinkle formation", may be quoted.
- **Texture and moisture are instrument results:** "measurably smoother texture from week 6" (register claim 5: not "visibly smoother"), and "higher
  skin moisture at weeks 8 and 9". The blinded smoothness ratings are not mentioned (amendment).
- **Wahab:** "In an independent double-blind trial, a serum with 2% glutathione and vitamin C lowered skin pigment by 10.8% in 8 weeks, while the
  placebo side did not fall (p = 0.029)." Always "with vitamin C" (the required qualifier: it is a pair, and the form of glutathione is not stated).
  Never attach its numbers to GSSG or to our serum. Its SPF-35 protocol supports our "with SPF in the morning".
- **Independence, said positively:** the independent evidence is Wahab (a trial) and the two academic reviews: topical 2% GSSG "could brighten skin
  color in sun-exposed area" (Dilokthornsakul 2019); topical glutathione "shows promise" (Khanna 2025). Never Khanna's numbers.
- **The manufacturer disclosure:** once in _At a glance_ ("run by Kyowa Hakko Bio, the company that makes GSSG") and in the Watanabe table row. This
  narrows fact 1's "wherever we cite it" to what the 2026-09-26 amendment allows.
- **Tolerability:** "no reactions attributed to glutathione in 76 women across two controlled trials" (Watanabe 30, Wahab 46; 8–10 weeks). No
  independent safety panel has assessed glutathione, so never imply one; "patch-test first" stays.
- **"Most abundant":** "the most abundant of the body's own sulphur-based antioxidants, found in every tissue" (Lu 2013). Not "the most abundant
  antioxidant" without that qualifier.
- **Sun:** never claim protection from sun-induced darkening (Muller 2024), and never frame glutathione as sun protection (Grandi).
- **Absorption:** never "absorbed", "penetrates" or "reaches the dermis". Say what was measured: applied to the skin, 2% GSSG lowered skin pigment from
  week 1.
- **Keeping it up:** say it positively ("use it for at least 10 weeks, and keep using it to keep the result"), not "the effect is temporary".
- **Products stay ingredient-level** until the certificate confirms 2% OXIDIZED GLUTATHIONE (§6 gap 1; memory
  `in-vivo-magnitudes-stay-off-products-until-the-formula-is-confirmed`).
