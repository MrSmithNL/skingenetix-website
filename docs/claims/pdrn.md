**Compiled:** 2026-09-22 · **Scope:** Skingenetix PDRN 1% Renewal Serum (30 ml) and PDRN 1% Collagen Night Cream (50 ml). The live product pages say both contain **1% PDRN (10,000 ppm)**. The cream's FAQ confirms 1%. Checked live on 2026-09-22.

## The one fact that governs every claim

**There is exactly one controlled human study of topical salmon PDRN on intact skin: Ye et al. 2026.** It used **0.1% PDRN-850K**, a medium-length PDRN (≈1,200 bp, ≤850 kDa, ≥96% pure, supplied by Ruijiming (Shandong) Biotechnology). It was an eye cream with PDRN as the only active. The comparator was **0.1% retinol in an identical base**. There was **no placebo or vehicle arm**. Every other human study is one of three things:
- injected (grade X)
- applied to skin opened by microneedling or a wound (not transferable to a leave-on on intact skin)
- a *different* "PDRN" from plants, algae or bacteria

So we can use:
1. **Ingredient-level clinical claims** ("In a clinical study, PDRN…"), with the concentration and study disclosed.
2. **Lab/ex vivo mechanism claims**, labelled as laboratory research.
3. **Product-level claims** limited to formulation facts and cosmetic benefits of the product as a whole (hydration from HA and beta-glucan, texture), **never** "our serum reduced wrinkles by X%".

We do **not** know whether our supplier's PDRN matches PDRN-850K in molecular weight. **Action: ask the supplier for the CoA and fragment size.** If it is medium-length (≈850 kDa or less), the Ye evidence transfers more credibly (still not a product claim).

## 3. Claims to AVOID

| Claim seen online or on our site | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Repairs DNA" / "repairs UV DNA damage" / "DNA repair" | **Medicinal** (a physiological action) and **in vitro only**: fibroblasts in culture at 100 µg/ml (Belletti 2007). No human topical data. |
| "Heals", "wound healing", "accelerates healing after lasers", "prevents scarring" | The wound and scar evidence is **medicinal and mostly injected** (Squadrito 2014; Kim 2023; Valdatta 2004 ointment on graft wounds). Post-procedure reviews admit the evidence is "indirect" (Flores Rodríguez 2026). |
| "Treats redness / rosacea / eczema / atopic skin" | Disease claims are medicinal. The redness data are **injected** (2-patient case report with PharmaResearch advisers) or **mouse** AD models. |
| "Fades acne scars" / "fades melasma / dark spots" | Melasma: the one controlled trial of topical PN was **null** (Gulfan 2022). Pigment data are in vitro, animal, or 6 injected patients. No topical human pigment study. |
| "Regenerates skin" / "stimulates stem cells" / "rebuilds collagen in your skin" | Medicinal tone, and the collagen evidence is ex vivo or in vitro. State "in laboratory studies on human skin samples". |
| "Topical PDRN reaches the dermis" | Porcine skin (FITC) only. In a living human it was **one volunteer**, signal in the *superficial viable epidermis*, ~3% relative, and Raman "cannot unequivocally distinguish exogenous PDRN from endogenous nucleic acids" (Ye 2026). |
| "The same results as injections / Rejuran in a bottle" | No head-to-head exists. |
| **"47% fewer fine lines, 39% more elasticity, 41% more hydration in 8 weeks (J Cosmet Dermatol 2023 split-face)"** and **"1% topical PDRN achieved 78% of injectable efficacy (52% vs 67% wrinkle depth, Korean Dermatological Society 2022)"** | Surfaced by a web-search summary. | "0.5–1% (5,000–10,000 ppm) is the clinically effective range" / "30–50% of topical PDRN reaches the dermis" | Circulates on brand blogs. | "Clinically proven" (unqualified) / "our serum is clinically proven" | No study of our formula. Use "clinically studied ingredient" plus the footnote. |
| "10× the concentration used in the clinical study" | Implies more is better. No dose-response data; different PDRN grade possible. Misleading under 655/2013. |
| "Salmon DNA is structurally similar to human DNA, ensuring excellent biocompatibility" (**live**, /pages/pdrn-research FAQ) | Unsourced pseudo-science: all DNA shares the double-helix chemistry. Biocompatibility of the drug grade rests on protein removal (Squadrito 2017), not on similarity. |

| "Well-documented safety profile" (**live**, pdrn-research + skin-repair-renewal) | The documented record (">300,000 prescriptions") is for the **injectable/registered drug**, not cosmetics. Topical human safety data = Ye (n=31) + Yogya (n=29, after microneedling). |

## 5. Gaps: what we'd need for stronger claims

1. **Supplier CoA and fragment size** for our Sodium DNA. If it is medium-length (≤850 kDa), our footnote can say "the same class of medium-length PDRN". If it is a different grade, Ye's relevance weakens.
2. **Our own consumer-perception study** (e.g. 30–50 users, 4 weeks, run by an independent CRO). This unlocks "91% said skin looked firmer*"-type claims attached **to the product**. It is the cheapest, highest-conversion upgrade, and standard under 655/2013 Annex III when it is properly worded.
3. **Our own instrumental study** (Corneometer hydration at 1 h / 24 h / 4 weeks; Cutometer; VISIA/Antera wrinkles; ideally vehicle-controlled split-face). A 4-week corneometer study alone would give "instantly +X% hydration" for the serum. The HA + beta-glucan in it make this likely to succeed.
4. **HRIPT / dermatologist-supervised in-use test** to unlock "dermatologically tested", "suitable for sensitive skin" and "non-comedogenic" on the product.
5. **A placebo/vehicle-controlled topical PDRN study** from the literature. None exists yet: Ye compared against retinol, not the base. Watch for one, and re-run this register in 6 months (PubMed query saved above: PDRN/PN AND topical AND randomized/split-face/placebo).
6. **Independent replication of Ye 2026.** It is a single study with commercial author affiliations. The claim is defensible as a sourced ingredient claim, but it is one study, so the footnote stays.
