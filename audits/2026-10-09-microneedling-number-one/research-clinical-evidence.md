## How to read the grades

The claims registers use A (clinical), C (ex vivo), D (cells). This file needs finer steps for microneedling, so it uses its own scale and says so:

| Grade | Meaning here                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A     | Randomised, controlled, objective outcome, in the use case we sell (facial, short needle, product like ours). **No study reaches this.**                            |
| B     | Randomised or split-face controlled human trial, but small, clinician-delivered, a different device or a different active. Or a systematic review pooling such work |
| C     | Uncontrolled human study, ex vivo or in-vitro skin work, case reports                                                                                               |
| D     | Animal, narrative review, expert opinion, or evidence the authors themselves rate as low                                                                            |

## 1. The headline finding

1. **There is no controlled trial of at-home, 0.5 mm-or-shorter microneedling for facial wrinkles using a stamp.** The nearest are one uncontrolled
   home study at 0.2 mm (n = 11), and clinician-applied 0.25 mm and 0.5 mm roller trials. A 2026 scoping review of home-use devices graded the home
   microneedling evidence **Level D and linked it to infectious complications** (Raef 2026, PMID 42545315, abstract only).
2. **Almost all the good microneedling trials are in a clinic at 1.2–2.5 mm, or use radiofrequency microneedling.** Those results do not transfer to a 0.5 mm stamp.
3. **Microneedling alone does something modest.** The two short-needle split-face trials disagree on whether the saline-plus-needle side improves (see Lee 2014 and Liang 2022).
4. **The add-on actives in those trials are not ours** (stem-cell conditioned media, growth factors, antioxidants). They show that "needle plus a good topical beats needle plus a bland one" can be demonstrated, not that PDRN or GHK-Cu does it.
5. **PDRN with microneedling: one wrinkle trial (Yogya) and one melasma trial (Gulfan), both radiofrequency microneedling in clinic. GHK-Cu with microneedling: no human trial.**

## 2. Reviews and meta-analyses

### R1. Foppiani 2025, Microneedling for Facial Rejuvenation: A Systematic Review

- **PMID 40542236** · DOI 10.1007/s00266-025-04972-z · _Aesthetic Plast Surg_ 2025 · abstract only (paywalled)
- **Design:** PRISMA systematic review plus a proportion meta-analysis of patient satisfaction. 346 records, **21 articles, 723 patients**; 72%
  female, mean age 48. Endpoints: wrinkling 71% of studies, texture 33%, photoaging 29%, laxity 14%. 90% (19/21) used multiple sessions with varying
  schedules.
- **Result:** pooled satisfaction **83%** (95% CI 0.76–0.88). Adverse effects: transient erythema 6.8%, scaling 1.7%, burning 1.5%, pruritus 0.4%.
- **Needle length:** not reported in the abstract and the full text was not accessible. **Whether the review separates home from clinic devices is unknown.**
- **Risk of bias:** the authors label it Level of Evidence IV and say the lack of standardised aesthetic outcome measures "warrants continued research". Satisfaction is a subjective endpoint with no control group.
- **Funding:** not seen.
- **What we may honestly say:** "A 2025 systematic review of 21 studies and 723 people found most were satisfied (83%) and side effects were mostly brief redness. The authors note outcome measures vary between studies and call for better research." Do not say it proves wrinkle reduction.

### R2. Ramaut 2018, Microneedling: Where do we stand now?

- **PMID 28690124** · DOI 10.1016/j.bjps.2017.06.006 · _J Plast Reconstr Aesthet Surg_ 2018 · abstract only
- **Design:** systematic review, PubMed and Google Scholar, many indications (scars, wrinkles, striae, alopecia, melasma). Some trials randomised and single-blind.
- **Result:** microneedling "showed noteworthy results" alone and with topicals or radiofrequency; similar to comparators with less downtime. The authors state "methodological shortcomings" and that more research is needed "to truly establish microneedling as an evidence-based" option.
- **Needle length / home split:** not in abstract.
- **What we may honestly say:** "Reviewers describe the research as promising but methodologically weak."

### R3. Alster and Graham 2018, Microneedling: A Review and Practical Guide

- **PMID 28796657** · DOI 10.1097/DSS.0000000000001248 · _Dermatol Surg_ 2018 · abstract only
- **Design:** narrative review plus the authors' clinical technique. **Grade D** (no systematic methods in the abstract).
- **Result:** concludes microneedling is "safe, minimally invasive, and effective" for scars, striae and rhytides.
- **What we may honestly say:** nothing beyond "dermatologists' practical guidance describes it as low-downtime". Do not cite it as efficacy evidence.

### R5. Kam 2026, Microneedling-Assisted Drug Delivery: Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

- **PMID 42664303** · DOI 10.1097/DSS.0000000000005315 · _Dermatol Surg_ 2026 · abstract only
- **Design:** systematic review and meta-analysis of **29 laboratory delivery studies** (Franz diffusion cells, histology, imaging). Not clinical outcomes.
- **Result:** microneedling increased delivery, standardised mean difference **4.61 (95% CI 2.99–6.24)**. Hydrophilic drugs more than hydrophobic (6.56 vs 3.49). **Needles ≥ 500 µm 5.96 vs < 500 µm 2.77 (p = 0.02).** **Applying the drug before microneedling 1.64 vs after 0.38 (p = 0.01).**
- **Why it matters to us:** it is the best pooled support for "a needle lets more of a water-soluble ingredient through". It also shows delivery is **smaller below 500 µm**, and that application order changes delivery. A 0.5 mm stamp sits right on the 500 µm threshold.
- **Caveat:** laboratory skin, not people; more delivered is not proven to mean better skin. The "before vs after" finding is a lab result and clashes
  with the safety reasoning in S1/S2 (pressing product into open channels). **This is a decision for Malcolm and the product instructions, not
  something to assert on a page.**
- **What we may honestly say:** "Across 29 laboratory studies, microneedle pre-treatment increased the amount of ingredient crossing skin, more so for water-loving molecules and for needles of 0.5 mm and longer. This was measured in the lab, not in people."

### R6. Home-device scoping review, Raef 2026

- **PMID 42545315** · DOI 10.1097/DSS.0000000000005252 · _Dermatol Surg_ 2026 · abstract only (paywalled; HTTP 402)
- **Design:** scoping review, PubMed, GRADE-rated. For facial rejuvenation: fractional non-ablative lasers Level A; LED and radiofrequency Level B; **"microneedling devices were supported by low-quality evidence (Level D) and associated with infectious complications"**.
- **What we may honestly say:** "A 2026 review of home devices rated the evidence for home microneedling as low quality." This is the most important honesty point in the file: we can say it plainly. The studies it included were not visible to me.

### R7. Context reviews (not appraised in depth)

- Exosomes: Dhaliwal 2026, **PMID 42027180**, _J Cosmet Dermatol_ (systematic review, 8 studies, sizes 3–60; "more evidence is required").
- GHK-Cu: Najafi 2026, **PMID 42787770**, _Arch Intern Med Res_ (systematic review, 64 records). Abstract states microneedle delivery of GHK-Cu "has
  yet to be evaluated in a single registered, adequately powered human randomized controlled trial". Low-prominence journal, abstract read only, but
  it agrees with our own register.
- RF microneedling for rejuvenation: Kumar 2026, **PMID 41947517** (20 studies, 558 people, mild transient effects). Radiofrequency, not our device.

## 3. Human trials: short needles (home-range, ≤ 0.5 mm)

### T1. Quinlan 2022, home microneedling plus topical growth factors

- **PMID 34951101** · DOI 10.1111/jocd.14650 · _J Cosmet Dermatol_ 2022 · abstract only (Wiley blocked)
- **Design:** prospective, single-centre, **uncontrolled**. **n = 11** healthy women aged 33–61, mild-moderate wrinkles.
- **Device / regimen:** **home-based 0.2 mm microneedling device twice weekly** plus twice-daily recombinant human growth-factor serum (SkinGenuity), 3 months.
- **Comparator:** none (before vs after).
- **Outcomes (VISIA, FACE-Q):** texture **+17.6%** (p < 0.001), wrinkles **+17.3%** (p < 0.001), red areas +12.4% (p = 0.004), brown spots +6.0% (p = 0.03); FACE-Q satisfaction up (all p ≤ 0.02). No serious adverse effects.
- **Risk of bias:** high. No control, so a serum alone, seasonal change, or the Hawthorne effect could explain it; n = 11; the serum is a branded
  product (SkinGenuity). **Funding / conflicts: not verified**; the Merati trial (T4) was funded by a different growth-factor maker, so check whether
  this one is too before quoting it.
- **Grade: C.**
- **What we may honestly say:** "In a small uncontrolled study, 11 women using a 0.2 mm home device twice a week with a growth-factor serum for 3 months showed improvements on skin-imaging scores and reported no serious problems." It cannot say the needle or the serum did it.

### T2. Lee 2014, 0.25 mm roller plus stem-cell conditioned medium

- **PMID 25324650** · DOI 10.5021/ad.2014.26.5.584 · _Ann Dermatol_ 2014 · **full text read** (PMC4198585)
- **Design:** randomised, observer-blinded, split-face. **n = 25** women, 41–64, Fitzpatrick III–IV.
- **Device / regimen:** **0.25 mm roller**, clinician-delivered; 5 sessions, 2 weeks apart; half-face hESC-EPC conditioned medium vs **saline**.
- **Outcomes:** periorbital wrinkles by skin replica (Visiometer R2 max roughness, R3 average roughness); pigment (Mexameter); physician global assessment.
- **Result (numbers):** conditioned-medium side R2 **0.58 → 0.46** (p < 0.05), R3 **0.40 → 0.31** (p < 0.05). **Saline + needle side R2 0.52 → 0.50 and R3 0.38 → 0.34, not significant** (R3 p = 0.51). Melanin index on the needle-alone side 143 → 136 (p = 0.052). One case of mild peeling.
- **Funding:** Korean government health R&D grant. No commercial funder seen.
- **Risk of bias:** n = 25, single follow-up two weeks after the last session, no long-term data (authors say so). Optimistic reading: 0.25 mm alone produced **no statistically significant wrinkle change** in this trial.
- **Grade: B.**
- **What we may honestly say:** "In a 25-woman split-face trial, five fortnightly sessions with a 0.25 mm roller plus a growth-factor-rich medium
  reduced eye-area roughness; the side with roller and saline did not change significantly." That second half is a useful honest counterweight to 'the
  needle does the work'.

### T3. Liang 2022, 0.5 mm roller plus umbilical-cord stem-cell conditioned medium

- **PMID 35685406** · DOI 10.3389/fmed.2022.837332 · _Front Med_ 2022 · **full text read** (PMC9171013)
- **Design:** randomised, controlled, split-face. 30 recruited, **28 completed**, mean age 41.
- **Device / regimen:** **0.5 mm dermaroller (192 needles)**, clinician-delivered; 5 sessions, 2 weeks apart; saline vs hUC-MSC conditioned medium.
- **Outcomes:** blinded dermatologists' ratings and VISIA wrinkles, pores, elasticity, brightness; satisfaction.
- **Result:** the conditioned-medium side beat saline on brightness, wrinkles, pores and elasticity (wrinkle change 12.15 vs 7.46, p reported as 0.00;
  the paper's wrinkle units are VISIA scores). **The saline + needle side also improved significantly on wrinkles** (reported p = 0.00 against its own
  baseline). No difference in hydration, water loss, redness. No severe side effects.
- **Funding:** Beijing Natural Science Foundation. No conflicts declared.
- **Risk of bias:** the dermatologists were blinded; the study did not report a sham-needle arm, so needle effect cannot be separated from the saline; p = 0.00 means "below display precision", not zero.
- **Grade: B.**
- **What we may honestly say:** "Five fortnightly sessions with a 0.5 mm roller improved eye-area wrinkles on both sides of the face, and more on the side that also received the stem-cell product."
- **Note:** T2 and T3 differ in needle length (0.25 vs 0.5 mm); the authors suggest this explains the difference between the two trials' needle-alone sides. Unproven.

### T4. Merati 2020, 0.5 mm pen plus growth-factor serum vs hyaluronic acid gel

- **PMID 33282098** · _J Clin Aesthet Dermatol_ 2020 · PMC7716740, page read
- **Design:** randomised controlled. **n = 20 (10 per arm)**, women 35–60. Blinding not described.
- **Device / regimen:** **motorised pen at 0.5 mm** (Rejuvapen), clinician-delivered; 4 sessions one month apart; PolyGF serum vs HA gel. 12 weeks.
- **Outcomes / result:** VISIA texture significantly better with PolyGF (p = 0.0156–0.0195); subject-rated hydration better (p = 0.0078–0.0156). The control (HA) group improved too. No adverse effects.
- **Funding:** **SkinGen, the maker of the growth-factor product, funded the study and sponsored an author.**
- **Grade: B−/C** (tiny arms, industry funded, no blinding).
- **What we may honestly say:** nothing about efficacy beyond "a small manufacturer-funded trial." Useful only as evidence that 0.5 mm pen sessions a month apart were well tolerated.

### T5. Jeong 2024, designing a placebo microneedle stamp (0.5 mm, 42 needles)

- **PMID 38543289** · DOI 10.3390/pharmaceutics16030395 · _Pharmaceutics_ 2024 · PMC10975904, page read
- **Design:** feasibility and blinding validation. **n = 15** healthy adults (9 aged 20–30, 6 aged 60–71). **Not an efficacy study.**
- **Device:** **0.5 mm stamp with 42 needles**, 0.6 kgf force; the placebo stamp has four blunt 1.0 mm acupressure-type pins and did not penetrate the skin (methylene-blue test).
- **Result:** pain with the real stamp **2.3 ± 1.9 /10** vs 1.7 ± 1.7 placebo. The real stamp was blinded successfully; the placebo was only partly blinded.
- **Funding:** Korean government health technology R&D (KHIDI).
- **Why it matters:** this is the only item that tests a **0.5 mm stamp** of our shape. It shows a sham-stamp design exists, which is how a proper trial could be run.
- **Grade: C** (device and tolerability only).
- **What we may honestly say:** "In a small study a 0.5 mm, 42-needle stamp caused mild pain, around 2 on a 10-point scale."

### T6. Zasada 2019, vitamin C with micro-needle mesotherapy (needle length not in abstract)

- **PMID 30070034** · DOI 10.1111/jocd.12727 · _J Cosmet Dermatol_ 2019 · abstract only
- **Design:** "preliminary randomised" split-face, **n = 17**. Four treatments of 20% L-ascorbic acid serum; left face no-needle mesotherapy (electroporation/ultrasound), right face micro-needle mesotherapy.
- **Result:** both worked; needle side "more efficient" for firmness, elasticity, hydration, tone. Numbers not in the abstract.
- **Grade: C.** Device and needle length unknown; do not place in the home column.
- **Say:** at most "a preliminary 17-person study found the needle side performed better for firmness and hydration with vitamin C".

### T7. Kamila 2020, ferulic peel plus microneedling

- **PMID 32654286** · DOI 10.1111/dth.14000 · _Dermatol Ther_ 2020 · abstract only
- **Design:** split-face, **n = 16** women 45–60; eight weekly sessions of 14% ferulic acid peel; right half also needled. Needle length not in abstract.
- **Result:** both sides improved on hydration, elasticity, melanin and redness (p < 0.05); the needled side improved more, especially elasticity. Numbers not in the abstract.
- **Funding:** PubMed lists "Research Support, Non-U.S. Gov't".
- **Grade: C.** **What we may say:** nothing strong. It is a peel, and weekly sessions.

### 5b. GHK-Cu / copper peptides

- **Human trials with microneedling: none found.** (Search: GHK-Cu OR copper peptide OR GHK AND microneedl\* OR needling OR dermaroller returned 7 records: reviews, the 2015 permeation study, a scalp tattooing paper.)
- **Li 2015 (PMID 25690343)**: ex vivo human skin, polymeric array; 134 ± 12 nmol of peptide in 9 h through needled skin vs "almost no" through intact
  skin; irritation tested in cells and pig skin only. Already in the register, **Grade C**. **Keep the register's caveat: amount, not depth; array,
  not stamp.**
- Najafi 2026 (R7) and the copper register both state the human gap.

### 5c. Other peptides (Matrixyl, Argireline)

- **No human trial of a roller, stamp or pen plus palmitoyl peptides or Argireline** was found.
- Nearest: **An 2019** (PMID 33911590, _Ann Dermatol_): double-blind randomised split-face, **52 Korean women (50 completed)**, a **dissolving
  hyaluronic acid microneedle patch** worn 4 h weekly for 29 days; acetyl hexapeptide-8 or EGF versions beat the patch alone on wrinkles (p < 0.05).
  **A different technology (a dissolving patch, not a stamp or roller). Grade B for the patch only.** Funding not seen.
- **Avcil 2020** (PMID 31134751): uncontrolled 12-week HA patch with several peptides; wrinkles "25.8% decrease", **no control, manufacturer product, patch.** Grade C.
- **What we may honestly say:** nothing for stamp-plus-peptide. We can say a peptide-loaded dissolving patch has shown a wrinkle benefit, and that this is a different product.

### 5d. Hyaluronic acid

- **Sundaram 2018** (PMID 29601621): split-face, 24 women (8 had microneedling, 8 filler, 8 peel); cross-linked HA serum from 2 days after the
  procedure improved hydration and texture vs untreated side at day 28; no adverse events. Only 8 people had microneedling; the serum is applied
  **after** healing begins, not into the channels. **Grade C.**
- **Arutinova 2026** (PMID 42830871, _Cureus_): 30 participants, non-randomised pilot, needling to **2 mm** with topical PLLA-HA vs needling alone; favoured the combination; authors cite no randomisation, allocation concealment or assessor blinding. **Grade C.**
- **Zasada 2019** (T6) and the **Merati** control arm (HA gel) show HA is used as a comparator rather than tested.

### 5e. Growth factors, conditioned media, exosomes (context)

- Rows T2, T3, T4, C3 and C5 above. Pattern: several small randomised split-face trials, mostly Asian cohorts, mostly Fitzpatrick III–IV, show an
  active beats saline on top of needling. The best two with short needles are Lee 2014 and Liang 2022. **None tests our ingredients.** A 2026
  systematic review of exosomes with microneedling (R7) concludes more evidence is required.

### S2. Trial adverse events

- Foppiani (R1): transient erythema 6.8%, scaling 1.7%, burning 1.5%, itch 0.4%.
- **Liu 2026 (C4): 2 of 31 withdrew with allergic contact dermatitis from the microneedling device** (nickel/metal allergy is one cause; Chu 2021 lists metal allergy as a risk factor). The study does not say which material.
- **Quinlan 2022 (T1):** no serious effects (n = 11). Lee 2014: mild peeling in one person. Merati: none.
- Jeong 2024: pain ~2/10 with the 0.5 mm stamp.

### S4. "How often" in the trials (nobody compared intervals)

| Study                 | Needle         | Schedule                                                                |
| --------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Quinlan 2022 (home)   | 0.2 mm         | **twice weekly for 3 months**, no serious effects, n = 11, uncontrolled |
| Lee 2014              | 0.25 mm roller | every 2 weeks, 5 sessions                                               |
| Liang 2022            | 0.5 mm roller  | every 2 weeks, 5 sessions                                               |
| Merati 2020           | 0.5 mm pen     | every 4 weeks, 4 sessions                                               |
| Liu 2026              | 1.2–1.5 mm     | every 4 weeks, 2 sessions                                               |
| El-Domyati 2015, 2018 | clinic         | every 2 weeks, 6 sessions                                               |

- **No randomised trial compares session intervals for facial rejuvenation.** Gowda 2021 reports no interval recommendation (page read via summariser).
- **What we may honestly say:** "In trials using needles up to 0.5 mm, sessions were usually 2 weeks apart; skin barrier tests show recovery within about 1–3 days." Anything shorter than every 2–3 days rests on one uncontrolled home study.

### S5. Contraindications named in the sources

Active infection or cold sores / herpes / shingles (Gowda, Chu, Leatham); darker skin and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation risk (Chu; and slower
pore closure, Ogunjimi); metal allergy (Chu; Liu); keloid or hypertrophic scar history (excluded in most trials per Gowda); products not approved for
intradermal use (Chu; Soltani-Arabshahi). Pregnancy and anticoagulants appear in practice guidance but **were not verified in any source I could
read**, so they are left out.

## 9. Decisions this surfaces for Malcolm (not acted on)

1. **Instruction order.** Kam 2026 finds lab delivery greater when the product is applied before needling. The safety literature (granulomas from
   non-intradermal products pushed in) argues for the opposite caution. Which order the stamp-set instructions use should be decided with the safety
   evidence beside the delivery evidence.
2. **A claim-safe sentence on home use.** The honest line is "no controlled trial of an at-home facial stamp yet; here is what the nearest studies show". That is also the content gap competitors leave.
3. **A trial opportunity.** Jeong 2024 shows a placebo stamp can be built. A split-face study with a sham stamp would be the first to answer the question, but this is a business decision.
4. **Safety block.** Active infection, cold sores, nickel allergy, darker-skin pigmentation risk, patch test, no oils, single-use vials, never share devices, with sources: Chu 2021, Gowda 2021, Soltani-Arabshahi 2014, Leatham 2018.
