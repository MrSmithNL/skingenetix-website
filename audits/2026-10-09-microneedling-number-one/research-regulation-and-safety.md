# Microneedling stamp sets: what is legally allowed and safe to say (US, EU, UK)

Researched 2026-10-09. Scope: Skingenetix sells two facial microneedling stamp sets (0.5 mm stamp + 4 single-use serum vials; one PDRN, one GHK-Cu). This note is research, not legal advice. Anything marked "my inference" is reasoning from the sources, not a regulator's statement.

**Evidence labels.** VERIFIED = I read the text at the source (or the regulator's own page/PDF) in this session. REPORTED = second-hand (trade press, law-firm or retailer pages, search-engine summary), or a source I could not open in full.

---

## 0. The five findings that matter most

1. **The 0.5 mm stamp is the weak point.** FDA draws the device line on _claims_ and on _whether the design lets needles reach living skin_; it sets
   no millimetre cut-off. Its own "not a device" examples are "short, blunt needles" or "short, densely packed needles... not designed to penetrate
   living layers of skin". A 0.5 mm sharp-needle stamp is not obviously in that category (my inference; stratum corneum is roughly 11-18 micrometres
   thick and the living epidermis another 57-82 micrometres at the sites measured by Sandby-Moller 2003, so 500 micrometres is many times the dead
   layer). Nothing I found names 0.3 mm (or any figure) as an official limit. The "0.3 mm cosmetic / 0.5 mm+ medical" rule is repeated by retailers
   and German health-info sites only.
2. **Telling customers to apply the vial right after stamping is the biggest US problem, not just a safety one.** FDA's guidance says microneedling
   products "promoted with topically applied substances... packaged together with the microneedling product or available separately where the
   microneedling product provides instructions for use with such topical products" "may be combination products" and are **outside the guidance's
   scope**. A stamp bundled with serum vials and "stamp, then apply" instructions is that scenario. FDA also says no microneedling device is cleared
   for use with another product (Section 1).
3. **US/EU/UK all key on what you claim.** "Stimulates collagen", "treats/reduces scars, wrinkles, acne", "promotes healing", "penetrates deeper
   layers" are the exact examples FDA gives for turning a stamp into a device. Appearance-only claims (smoother look, luminous, exfoliation) are the
   permitted set in the US, and the UK/Dutch/EU sources point the same way.
4. **EU: no microneedling entry exists in the places I could check.** MDR Annex XVI (products without a medical purpose) lists six groups and
   microneedling is not one; the Commission's borderline manual (Version 5, April 2026) has no microneedling entry. So in the EU the answer is the
   ordinary MDR test (intended medical purpose, set by the manufacturer's labelling and promotion) versus the Cosmetics Regulation / general product
   safety law. No national BfArM or IGJ/NVWA statement specific to home stamps was found.
5. **Serum on freshly needled skin: no regulator or trade body gave a "this is fine" position, and the EU Cosmetics Regulation is written for intact
   skin.** The SCCS (EU cosmetics scientific committee) states it prefers dermal-absorption data from intact skin and has no standard protocol for
   damaged skin. Peer-reviewed case reports (Soltani-Arabshahi 2014; Huang 2025) show granulomas and hypersensitivity when topical products meet
   needled skin, including at home. The defensible posture is in Section 5.

### 1.2 The 2020 guidance: when a stamp is a device

[FDA, _Regulatory Considerations for Microneedling Products_, Guidance for Industry and FDA Staff, issued 10 November 2020 (draft 15 September 2017),
Docket FDA-2017-D-4792](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/regulatory-considerations-microneedling-products)
(PDF read in full: <https://www.fda.gov/media/107708/download>). VERIFIED. Non-binding.

- Scope explicitly names "microneedle stamps, dermal stamps, dermal rollers". Generic term covers needles "blunt or sharp, and of varying lengths", single- or multi-use.
- Device test = intended use under FD&C Act 201(h): diagnosis/treatment of disease, **or "intended to affect the structure or any function of the body"**. Intent comes from the firm's labelling, advertising and promotion, "and from other relevant sources".
- "**FDA considers claims or statements that indicate penetration or some effect beyond the stratum corneum into living layers of skin... to be evidence of a firm's intent to affect the structure or function of the body.**" Removal of the dead stratum corneum is not.
- Claims FDA says "would generally cause the product to meet the device definition": treats scars (acne, atrophic, hypertrophic, burn); treats
  wrinkles and deep facial lines; treats cellulite and stretch marks; treats dermatoses; treats acne; treats alopecia; **stimulates collagen
  production**; stimulates angiogenesis; **promotes wound healing**.
- Design factors FDA may weigh even without claims: "Needle length and arrangement and whether the specifications facilitate penetration into living
  layers of skin"; needle sharpness; control over depth. "Information regarding design... may be found in... the product's specifications, directions
  for use, or other materials."
- Not-a-device examples (Section V and Q&A 2): products "that do not penetrate living skin" and claim only exfoliation, "improve the appearance of
  skin", "smoother look and feel", "luminous look"; example products have "short, blunt needles" or "short, densely packed needles that are not
  designed to penetrate living layers of skin". Even then the product "may still be subject to other requirements of the FD&C Act or other Federal
  statutes".
- Q&A 1(a): a manual product with **short, blunt needles** that claims "exfoliate, luminous look, **stimulate collagen production**, and treat wrinkles" IS a device. One collagen claim defeats the exfoliation claims.
- **Combination products:** products "promoted with topically applied substances such as creams, ointments, gels, vitamin solutions, drugs... packaged
  together... or available separately where the microneedling product provides instructions for use with such topical products... may be combination
  products under 21 CFR 3.2(e)... outside the scope of this guidance; manufacturers... should contact the Office of Combination Products".
- Classification: aesthetic microneedling devices are Class II, 21 CFR 878.4430, 510(k) required. "This classification does not include devices
  intended for transdermal delivery of topical products such as cosmetics, drugs, or biologics." Special controls target "adverse tissue reaction;
  cross-contamination and infection... damage to underlying tissue... scarring, and hyper/hypopigmentation due to exceeding safe penetration depth".
- The guidance contains **no needle-length figure** (I searched the full text for 0.3 / 0.25 / mm values: none).

### 1.3 Cosmetic vs drug side (the serum)

[FDA, _Is It a Cosmetic, a Drug, or Both? (Or Is It
Soap?)_](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap) (read 2026-10-09). VERIFIED. Intended use is
set by labelling, advertising and consumer perception; drug claims include "restore hair growth, reduce cellulite... increase or decrease the
production of melanin... **or regenerate cells**"; the law has no "cosmeceutical" category. This matters for PDRN and GHK-Cu copy: "regenerates",
"repairs tissue", "boosts collagen" are drug-type structure/function claims.

### 1.4 The 2025 RF microneedling communication

[FDA Safety Communication, _Potential Risks with Certain Uses of Radiofrequency (RF) Microneedling_, 15 October

VERIFIED (via page summary; I did not re-read the full text). Burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement, nerve damage reported; "RF microneedling is a
medical procedure, not a cosmetic treatment"; should not be used at home. Relevance to Skingenetix: **none directly (non-RF stamp)**, but it shows
FDA's current, tougher tone on microneedling in the consumer space. Do not use "RF" wording. AAD responded 16 October 2025 ([AAD
statement](https://www.aad.org/news/statement-on-fda-rf-microneedling-risks), VERIFIED).

**GPSR (Regulation (EU) 2023/988), Article 2:** applies to products placed on the market "to the extent that there are no specific provisions in Union
law" with the same safety objective; excludes medicines, food, feed, etc. Read via [gesetze.legal (German text
mirror)](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2023_988/2), REPORTED (not the official EUR-Lex copy). Practical consequences (risk analysis, responsible
person in the EU, safety information, traceability) are known to apply from 13 December 2024 but I did not verify that date or the labelling duties at
source this session.

**What wording turns a stamp into an EU medical device?** No EU authority publishes a list. By analogy with the MDR Article 2(1) definition,
"treats/reduces scars, stretch marks, wrinkles, acne", "heals", "repairs", "stimulates collagen/regeneration/wound healing", "remodels skin" state a
medical purpose or a modification of anatomy or a physiological process. A Dutch government source (below) and the German retail health sites agree in
principle, but none is a legal list. Flag: this is inference.

**Reading (my inference):** the EU framework treats a cosmetic as something applied to the _external_ surface, and its safety assessment is built on
intact-skin absorption data. A serum labelled "use immediately after stamping" is, in effect, a product intended for application to a deliberately
broken barrier. It still _can_ be a cosmetic, but it needs a safety assessment (CPSR) that explicitly models that use, and the SCCS has said it will
not rely on damaged-skin data as primary evidence. That is a gap you must close with your own safety assessor, not something a regulator has blessed.

  12 Feb 2026, in force 13 Feb 2026 (VERIFIED, extract). Schedule 1 item 6 covers "A procedure in which microneedles are used to puncture skin to a
  depth of **less than 1.5 millimetres**" (excluding RF microneedling). Providers need a licence from 6 September 2027. It regulates the provision of
  procedures, not sale of home devices. A separate Scottish Bill on higher-risk procedures (deeper microneedling) is reported ([Mishcon / MFMac,
  REPORTED](https://www.mfmac.com/insights/healthcare-lifesciences/scotlands-beauty-sector-set-for-a-2027-makeover/)) and was not read at source.
- Practical meaning for Skingenetix: customers doing it at home are outside these licensing rules, but copy that sounds like a "treatment" or a "procedure" invites the comparison. Say "skin-care tool", not "treatment".

## 4. Sterility and safety: what dermatology bodies and the literature say

### 4.1 Dermatology bodies

- **AAD (VERIFIED), [Microneedling can fade scars... , last updated
  7/17/23](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/scars-stretch-marks/microneedling-fade-scars):** "Products sold for at-home microneedling and
  microneedling performed in a non-medical spa aren't meant to puncture the skin." Risks: overuse irritates; "Sometimes an at-home microneedling
  product pierces the skin. This could lead to an infection or scarring."; "At-home microneedling can accidentally spread warts, herpes, and other
  viruses across the skin."; pressing too hard can scar or change colour/texture. No needle-length figure.
- **BAD (British Association of Dermatologists):** **no BAD page on home microneedling or derma rollers found.** A search summary attributed detailed
  needle-length and "post-procedure serums formulated for open skin" statements to BAD, but I could not trace them to any BAD page, so I do not rely
  on them. Do not cite BAD.
- **FDA** (Section 1) is the only regulator with a patient safety list; it applies to authorised professional devices, "re-use of the needle cartridge is unsafe... even if the cartridge is cleaned" (FDA Microneedling Devices page), and says home users should clean between uses and not share.

  for home, 0.5 mm+ professional" convention comes from retailers, health-info sites and a search summary of the American Society for Dermatologic

- **Single-use vs reusable heads / sterilisation:** FDA (professional context) treats cartridge reuse as unsafe and notes cross-contamination and

  advice from FDA is only "clean between uses as directed by the manufacturer" and "do not share".

**Market notes**

- **US:** an appearance-only stamp with short, non-penetrating needles can be a non-device; a 0.5 mm stamp sold with serum and "stamp then apply"
  instructions risks being treated as a device or combination product. The strict reading is FDA's own. The practical safe harbours are: strip the
  claims, strip the pairing instruction, or take the question to FDA (Office of Combination Products, or a formal classification request; the latter
  mechanism is from general knowledge, not verified here).
- **EU:** claims drive device status (MDR Art 2(1)); stay out of Annex XVI language ("introduction" into the dermis). The serum must have a CPSR under
  1223/2009; the stamp needs GPSR compliance (EU responsible person, safety info, traceability; details not verified at source). French and Belgian
  professional-only rules exist for practitioners, not for consumer stamps; check before shipping to FR/BE.
- **UK:** MHRA test is manufacturer's claims and mode of action. ASA evidence bar is high in 2026. Under-18 targeting rule exists for "cosmetic interventions"; check whether to treat the stamp ad the same way (not confirmed).

### 5.2 Does recommending the serum immediately after stamping create a problem?

**Yes: a regulatory problem in the US and a safety/assessment problem everywhere, unless you change or substantiate the instruction.**

1. **US combination product.** FDA treats microneedling products "promoted with topically applied substances... where the microneedling product
   provides instructions for use with such topical products" as possible combination products, outside its guidance; no microneedling device is
   cleared for use with another product; the combination's risks "are not known". The set as described (stamp + vials + "apply after stamping") sits
   squarely in that sentence. Source: FDA guidance Section II; FDA Consumer Update; FDA Microneedling Devices page. VERIFIED.
2. **Safety evidence points the wrong way.** Granulomas and sensitisation after products meet needled skin (Soltani 2014, including a branded vitamin
   C moisturiser patch-test positive; Huang 2025 at-home device plus oil). Rollers open channels that let more material through (Badran 2009), and the
   order matters (Chung 2020: product present at needling goes deeper). VERIFIED abstracts.
3. **EU/UK cosmetics law is written for intact skin.** SCCS prefers intact-skin absorption data; Annex I asks for particular microbiological attention on damaged skin. No body has said cosmetics are fine on freshly needled skin. VERIFIED, with the absence of a pro-use position being an honest gap.
4. **PDRN and GHK-Cu specifics** (not researched here beyond one note): PDRN is salmon-derived DNA, so protein/DNA-fragment sensitisation and fish
   allergy are plausible hazards in a barrier-breached setting (my inference, no source read), and copper peptides add metal sensitisation questions.
   The assessor should check both.

**Defensible posture, strongest first (all my inference from the sources; none is a regulator-approved wording):**

- **A. Decouple the serum from the stamp (lowest risk).** Sell the stamp for exfoliation/smoother look only; sell the serum as an ordinary cosmetic
  "for intact skin"; the leaflet and pages never instruct to apply the serum onto stamped skin, never pair them in one "how to use" sequence, and warn
  "do not apply any product to skin that is broken, bleeding or very red". Removes the FDA combination-product trigger and the broken-skin assessment
  problem.
- **B. If you keep the pairing, make it a properly assessed claim.** Before saying it: (i) a cosmetic safety assessment that explicitly includes
  "applied to skin immediately after micro-puncture" as an exposure scenario (compromised barrier, higher absorption), signed by a qualified assessor
  (CPSR for EU/UK); (ii) microbiological challenge and a sterile, preservative-appropriate single-dose format with batch sterility testing; (iii)
  human patch testing and an allergen review of PDRN and copper peptide; (iv) a documented position on whether the 0.5 mm stamp is a device, ideally
  confirmed with FDA and an EU notified-body/competent-authority query; (v) wording such as "use on clean, intact skin; stop if stinging or redness
  persists" instead of "designed for use after microneedling". Expect the FDA question to remain open.
- **C. Do not use** "sterile" or "designed for use after microneedling" as marketing phrases until (ii) and (iii) exist on file.
- **D. Hard stops either way:** single user, replace the stamp per the manufacturer's schedule, no sharing, no use over cold sores, active acne, rash, eczema, sunburn, or on blood thinners (FDA list; AAD infection and viral spread warning).
